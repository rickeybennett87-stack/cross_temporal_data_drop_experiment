# hve_ref_scaffold_words.md
# Human Voice Enforcement — Rule 10 Reference: Scaffold Words
# Companion to: human-voice-enforcement.md

Transitional scaffolding announces the connection between ideas instead of carrying it.
If the sentences are well-constructed, the connection is already present.
Delete the scaffold. If the connection disappears, fix the sentences.

## Flagged Scaffold Words and Phrases

- Furthermore
- Moreover
- In addition
- Additionally
- In conclusion
- To conclude
- To that end
- With that said
- With that in mind
- As such (when used as a transition, not as a predicate)
- It is thus the case that
- It is therefore clear that
- It is hence evident that
- Building on this
- Taking this into account
- Given the above
- In light of the foregoing
- Having said that (as opener, not as genuine concession)

## Permitted Transitions

These carry genuine logical content and are not scaffold:

- **However** — genuine contrast
- **But** — genuine contrast (prefer this to "however" in most cases)
- **Because** — causal connection
- **So** — consequence (informal but honest)
- **Yet** — genuine tension
- **Still** — persistence despite previous claim
- **Then** — genuine temporal or logical sequence
- **Though** / **Although** — genuine qualification

## The Test
Remove the word. Does the logical connection between the sentences survive?
If yes: the scaffold was doing nothing. Leave it out.
If no: the word was carrying genuine logical content. It is not scaffold — keep it.
