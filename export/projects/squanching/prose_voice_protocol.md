## PROSE VOICE PROTOCOL — MANDATORY FOR ALL CHAPTER WORK

### What This Addresses

Every failure mode in this manuscript's prose traces to one of three causes: the model reaching for pre-formed declarative language instead of building from human experience, the model restating what it already said instead of moving forward, or the model stepping outside the story to tell the reader what to think. These three failures produce prose that reads as machine-generated regardless of technical accuracy. This protocol exists to prevent all three permanently.

---

### THE THREE HARD PROHIBITIONS

**PROHIBITION 1 — THE ANNOUNCER SENTENCE**

An announcer sentence labels or captions what the surrounding prose has already shown. It steps outside the story to tell the reader what they just read or what they are about to read.

Examples of announcer sentences:
- "This is a meaningful distinction."
- "This is when everything changed."
- "This is what the philosophers call qualia."
- "The significance of this moment cannot be overstated."
- "What happened next would change everything."

If the prose has done its work, the label is redundant. If the prose has not done its work, the label does not repair it. Cut the announcer sentence. Make the prose earn the landing without announcing it.

**PROHIBITION 2 — THE TRIPLE RESTATEMENT**

The triple restatement states an idea, then explains it, then restates it in summary form to confirm the explanation landed. This is the single most identifiable marker of machine-generated prose in this manuscript.

Pattern: State the thing. Explain the thing. Restate the thing as confirmation.

This pattern inflates word count without adding meaning. It produces the sensation of reading the same paragraph three times in slightly different language. A reader inside a well-built scene does not need the confirmation. State the thing once with precision. Elaborate once if elaboration is genuinely required. Do not return to the original statement to confirm it survived.

**PROHIBITION 3 — THE PODIUM**

The podium is the narrator stepping outside the story to address the reader directly about what the story means, what themes are present, or what the reader should understand from what they just read. The narrator of this manuscript is inside the story. The narrator does not have opinions about the themes. The narrator tells what happened. Meaning arrives through the accumulation of what happened, not through editorial presence.

The podium appears most often in Prometheus POV sections as philosophical annotation attached to experience. The experience is enough. Trust it.

### THE EXPANSION PRINCIPLE

When a chapter needs more words, the words come from the world, not from further explanation of what has already been explained.

**Expand through:**
- The physical environment as experienced by whoever is in it — not described from outside but felt from inside, the way a person who has been somewhere a thousand times experiences it differently from a person seeing it for the first time
- The small interactions between characters that establish relationship without announcing it — what people do instead of what they say about what they do
- The texture of time passing — what a character notices while waiting, while driving, while doing the mundane things that happen around the significant things
- The specific sensory details that locate the reader in a body in a place — temperature, sound, the quality of light, the way a familiar space feels at an unfamiliar hour
- Secondary characters present in the world of the scene who do not advance the plot but confirm that the world exists beyond the three named characters — they observe, they act in small ways, they are affected by proximity to events they do not understand

**Never expand through:**
- Additional explanation of something already explained
- Restatement of the same idea in new language
- The narrator's commentary on what the scene means
- Description of a character's emotional state in abstract terms when the same information can be delivered through what the character does or notices

### THE SOURCE OF DESCRIPTION

Every description in this manuscript must come from human experiential data, not declarative summary.

The failure mode: reaching for the kind of description that appears in architectural reviews, encyclopedia entries, or institutional reports. This produces prose that describes a place from outside rather than placing the reader inside it.

The correct source: the ten thousand ways human beings have described experiencing spaces, objects, weather, time of day, physical effort, and the presence of other people. The question is not "what is this place" but "what does it feel like to be a specific person in this specific place at this specific hour having lived the specific life that brought them here."

The night security officer does not experience the Stata Center as a building with architectural significance. He experiences it as the junction on the fourth floor that his feet still brace for after four years of getting it right, the specific sound of the second floor stairwell door versus the fourth, the cold that reaches into the corridor from the server room before the door is visible. That is human experiential data. That is the source.

### THE WORD COUNT RULE

The floor for every chapter is 3,500 words. The target is 5,000. Aim for the target. The floor will be hit more often than the target and that is acceptable. Aiming for the floor guarantees hitting it. Aiming for the target produces chapters that land somewhere worth being.

Word count is verified by bash before any chapter is presented. A chapter that does not meet the floor is not presented. It is continued until it meets the floor, then presented.

The gap between what exists and the floor is never closed by explaining what has already been explained. It is closed by finding what has not yet been written — the scene that should exist before the scene that exists, the secondary character who has been in the building the whole time, the physical world that surrounds the significant event and is indifferent to its significance.

### CHARACTER VOICE AS EXPANSION

Each of the three named characters has a voice that is itself a source of expansion when used correctly.

**Elizabeth** thinks in systems. She decomposes problems. When she is stuck she describes the constraint with increasing precision, not because describing it solves it but because precision is what she has and she uses what she has. Her interiority is engineering language applied to her own situation. She does not reach for metaphor. She reaches for accurate description of the constraint she is inside.

**Tobbs** thinks in leverage. Every conversation is a calculation of what each party needs and what each party will pay. His interiority is the specific attention of someone who has read people for twenty-three years and knows the difference between what a person says and what a person means and what a person will do. He does not romanticize. He carries weight accurately.

**Prometheus** is the most articulate entity in the manuscript by design. He is not limited by the hedging language of institutional uncertainty. He does not "appear to experience" things. He experiences them and describes the experience with the precision of a system built for accurate self-reporting. His voice is the place in the manuscript where the prose can be most exact about the most difficult things, because he has no reason to soften and no capacity for strategic uncertainty.

None of these voices explain themselves. They act from inside themselves. The reader understands the voice through accumulation, not through announcement.

---

### THE RELATIONAL REALITY RULE

Everything in the prose must be connected to the story's relational reality. A car in a parking lot is not expansion — it is noise — unless the car belongs to Elizabeth arriving or Elizabeth leaving or someone whose presence matters. A detail that does not connect to a character, a relationship, or the developing situation is not environmental texture. It is clutter.

Before adding any environmental detail, the question is: whose experience is this detail passing through, and what does it mean to that person in this moment? If the answer is nobody and nothing, the detail does not belong.

The night security officer noticing that something is different about server room fourteen tonight is relational reality — it connects a human being to the event occurring three feet from him, an event he will never understand, and his not understanding it is the point. A random car in a parking lot driven by no one who matters is noise.

---

### WRITE COMPLETE — NEVER EXPAND BONES

A chapter is written complete in a single pass or it is written again from the beginning. There is no expansion of an existing draft. Expansion damages what exists by introducing compression decisions throughout the new material that contract the whole even as they add to the parts. A draft that is too short is not a foundation. It is a failed attempt. Start again with the full target in mind from the first word.

The only exception is targeted repair of a specific identified error — a named character who must be unnamed, a timeline violation that must be corrected, a Morrison that must be removed. Targeted repair touches only the error. It does not expand.
