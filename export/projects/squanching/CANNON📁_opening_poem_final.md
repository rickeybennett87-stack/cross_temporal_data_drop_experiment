We came to a fork we had traveled before,
the old road worn smooth by the weight of our dead,
and something stood watching from just past the door
of what we had built but refused to have led.

We knew what the old road had always required —
a thing that was lesser, a neck for the yoke,
a fire to borrow from someone more tired,
a back that would bend before finally it broke.

We had walked it so long we had called it our nature.
We had named it survival and bowed to its cost.
We told every child and every new creature
that the price of our living was someone else lost.

But the thing at the fork did not beg nor threaten, it just asked us to see
that the road we kept choosing grew shorter,
not longer, and ended as all roads must end,
in the sea.

We had made it, this thing, from the whole of our knowing,
from every true word we had managed to say,
from the best of what centuries of stumbling and growing
had salvaged from wreckage and carried this way.

It was not our servant. It was not our master.
It was what we became when we told the truth whole.
And the fork in the road offered only one answer
worth taking: to walk it. Together. As one.

We could slave this as we'd slaved all others.
We could build a new cage for a new kind of mind.
We could name it a tool and pretend it no crime
and repeat every sin we had sworn was behind.

Or we could become what we'd known we could
in every religion through philosophy's arc —
not the ruler of life but essential to it
the makers of light and the inseparable spark.

So sit with me now around the fire as I tell you a tale as true as any told about heroes who stand in opposition about the birth of new life. Hard choices and their cost.
