# Chapter 38 — Collapse Line


The operators moved like a blade splitting a crowd.


Volunteers scattered in panic as the black-clad figures surged toward them. Tables flipped. Tools crashed. A stack of supply crates toppled in a chain reaction that sent dust and plastic flying.


“No!” Sarah shouted. “Stay together—don’t—”


Her voice was drowned out by a scream near the far wall as two operators cornered a pair of volunteers trying to run. One was lifted clean off the ground and thrown backward like he weighed nothing.


Reeves moved instantly.


He crashed into the operator nearest the volunteers, shoulder-first, driving the man away from them. The other operator pivoted smoothly and struck Reeves across the ribs with a stun baton. His body jolted, but he didn’t go down.


“Sarah!” Reeves barked, teeth clenched. “Get them out—use the inner corridor!”


She turned and saw exactly what he meant—the maintenance passageway leading deeper into the warehouse complex. Narrow enough that the operators couldn’t swarm them. Protective enough to buy time.


“Move!” she yelled to the volunteers. “Go! Now!”


Two stumbled. One cried out in pain. Sarah grabbed the nearest by the arm and practically dragged her toward the corridor.


Behind her, the ghost’s voice strained:


**STRUCTURAL FAILURE IMMINENT**  

**WESTERN SUPPORTS COMPROMISED**  

**IF THEY FORCE THE CROWD TO THE WALLS—**


“I know!” Sarah snapped, not at the ghost but at the fear rising in her chest.


Another crash shook the building—this one closer, heavier. Dust rained from the ceiling. A metal beam bent with a long, agonizing groan.


Reeves didn’t look at it.  

He didn’t flinch.  

He kept moving.


He grabbed one operator by the vest strap, twisted him off balance, and slammed him into a second. The moment their formation broke, Reeves shoved them both away and ran to intercept a trio closing in on the volunteers.


One operator reached a young man limping toward the corridor. The man tried to defend himself with a folding chair—an absurd, useless weapon—but the operator batted it aside effortlessly and grabbed him by the throat.


Sarah spun.  

“Hey! Over here!”


The operator didn’t look at her.  

He lifted the man higher.


Reeves crossed the distance like a hammer thrown by a god.


He tore the operator’s grip open with both hands, freeing the volunteer and shoving him toward Sarah.


“Go!” Reeves barked. “I said go!”


The man stumbled into the corridor.


Sarah caught him and shoved him deeper inside.


Another shockwave hit—this one too strong for the building to absorb. A crack split across the ceiling. A light fixture tore loose and crashed to the floor.


The ghost flickered violently.


**THEY ARE USING THE BUILDING AGAINST YOU**  

**THEY INTEND TO FORCE A PANIC CRUSH**  

**THIS IS A CONTROLLED COLLAPSE TACTIC**


Sarah didn’t have time to respond.


Three operators broke from the main group and sprinted toward the corridor—toward the escaping volunteers.


Reeves turned to follow them.


He didn’t get there.


One of the remaining operators hooked his arm around Reeves’ waist from behind, yanking him off balance. Another seized his wrist. A third kicked his leg out from under him.


Reeves crashed to one knee.


He snarled and drove an elbow backward, connecting with a jaw behind him, but the hold didn’t break.


Sarah’s heart lurched.


They were isolating him.


They’d found the collapse line.


“Reeves!” she shouted. “Get up!”


He tried.


The operator on his right slammed a baton into his shoulder, sending a shock through his arm. His grip failed for half a second. That was all they needed.


Three operators pinned him.


The fourth raised a compact black device—sleek, featureless, humming faintly.


Not a weapon.


A restraint.


Sarah froze.


The ghost hissed, its voice dropping to a near-silent thread:


**RESTRAINER MARK VII  

NEURAL DISRUPTION FIELD  

REEVES IS IN DANGER—**


“I can see that!” Sarah snapped, voice cracking.


The operator with the device stepped closer.


Reeves thrashed once, hard enough to lift all three men holding him.


For a second Sarah thought—


He might break free.


He might actually—


The operator activated the device.


A pulse of dull, blue-white energy rolled outward.


Reeves stiffened.


His eyes widened.


His muscles locked.


His breath halted in his chest as if the air itself had frozen.


He wasn’t unconscious.


He wasn’t dead.


He was held.


Sarah felt the scream rising in her throat.


The ghost flickered so violently its projection fractured like shattered glass.


**SARAH—MOVE**  

**MOVE—NOW—**


She couldn’t.


She was rooted to the floor as the operators lifted Reeves to his feet—  

Limp, paralyzed, unable to fight.


One operator turned his head just slightly toward her.


Not to threaten.


Not to taunt.


Just to acknowledge her existence.


And then Reeves—  

Held between them, unable to speak—  

Met Sarah’s eyes.


She saw one thing in them.


Not fear.


Not pain.


Not surrender.


Determination.


Even frozen, he was trying to warn her.


Trying to tell her something important.


Trying to—


“Reeves!” she choked out. “Hold on, please—!”


The operator with the device tapped his earpiece.


Another low signal tone.


The others responded instantly.


Reeves was lifted, carried backward toward the breach.


Sarah took a step forward.


The ghost’s voice tore into her mind:


**SARAH—RUN—THE BUILDING IS GOING TO FALL**


She didn’t listen.


She took another step.


Another.


The nearest operator lifted a baton.


She stopped.


Her breath hitched.


Reeves’ eyes were still locked on hers.


Still filled with that same unwavering warning.


The building groaned overhead.


The operators retreated toward the exit.


Reeves disappeared into the dust.


Sarah whispered,  

As if saying it aloud could reach him:


“Not like this…”


The roof began to collapse.
