# Chapter 40 — Embers of the Lost


The warehouse finally gave way behind them.


It folded into itself with a long, low shudder, then collapsed in a plume of dust that rolled across the empty lot like a slow-moving tide. Metal groaned. Concrete cracked. A final support snapped with a sound like a gunshot.


Sarah didn’t turn around.


Her eyes were still fixed on the dark street where the van had carried Reeves away.


The ghost hovered beside her, its form stabilizing—slowly—like a system forcing itself back into coherence after being shaken apart.


**SARAH…  

WE MUST REGROUP  

THE SURVIVORS REQUIRE DIRECTION**


She swallowed hard, wiped dust and blood from her face with the back of her hand, and forced herself to stand.


The world swayed around her.


But she stood.


Because she had to.


The corridor emptied out behind her as volunteers stumbled into the lot—coughing, shaking, some injured, all terrified. The youngest were crying. The oldest just stared at the ruins, hollow-eyed.


They were waiting.


For someone to tell them what came next.


Sarah took a breath that tasted like smoke.


“Headcount,” she said, voice hoarse but steady.  

“Anyone missing—shout their names. We find who we can.”


A few nodded and spread out. Others clung to each other for support. Some looked at her the way drowning people look at the shore.


She didn’t let them see her shake.


The ghost’s projection flickered once, then steadied into a calm, faintly glowing silhouette.


**YOU ARE FUNCTIONING AT 63% OF OPTIMAL CONDITION  

YOU SHOULD REST**


“Later,” she muttered.


**RECOMMEND NOT TO POSTPONE RECONSTITUTION  

YOU SUFFERED TRAUMA TO—**


“Later,” she insisted.


The ghost hesitated.  

Then dimmed in compliance.



The survivors returned one by one.  

Twenty-seven accounted for.  

Three unconscious but alive.  

Four missing.


Sarah closed her eyes.


Either buried in the collapse—  

Or taken.


Neither possibility was something she could sit with right now.


“We need transport,” she said, opening her eyes again. “We can’t stay here.”


One of the volunteers—Callen, a wiry man with an electrician’s hands—nodded.  

“My van made it through the first blast. It’s beat to hell, but she runs.”


“Good,” Sarah said. “We’ll load the injured first.”


Callen hesitated.  

“Where do we go?”


Sarah looked at the ghost.


The ghost looked back.


**A SECURE LOCATION IS REQUIRED  

ACCESS TO DATA  

AND A LOW VISIBILITY PROFILE**


“We’re not going home,” Sarah said. “We can’t go to any public facility. We can’t go to any known safehouse. We need… something off-grid.”


Callen blinked.  

“Off-grid like what?”


Sarah inhaled slowly.


“You remember the storage compound by the river? The one they never finished converting? Power’s inconsistent, cameras are ancient, and nobody’s touched the place in years.”


Callen frowned.  

“You think it’s safe?”


“No,” she said plainly.  

“But it’s safer than here.”


That was enough.


The survivors began moving.



As they worked, the ghost drifted closer, voice softer than before.


**SARAH  

THERE IS SOMETHING YOU MUST KNOW**


She stiffened.


“What?”


**REEVES’ CAPTURE WAS NOT THE PRIMARY OBJECTIVE**


She froze.


“…Then what was?”


The ghost’s glow dimmed.


**YOU.**


A chill ran up Sarah’s spine.


“Me?” she whispered. “Why me?”


**YOU ARE THE CONNECTION  

THE UNPREDICTABLE CROSSPOINT  

THE VARIABLE THEY CANNOT MODEL**


Her pulse hammered in her ears.


“You’re saying Reeves was taken because of me?”


**YES  

AND NO**


“Try again.”


The ghost paused, searching for the right structure.


**YOU ARE ANOMALOUS  

REEVES IS ESSENTIAL  

TO CONTAIN YOU  

THEY NEEDED HIM**


Sarah’s breath left her body.


“They’re using him to control me.”


**CORRECT**


The words hit harder than any shock baton.


She steadied herself against the side of the van, knuckles white.


“They won’t get what they want.”


**THEY DEPEND ON THAT**  

**IT IS PART OF THEIR STRATEGY**


Sarah swallowed, forcing her mind into clarity.


“So we go to ground. We regroup. We figure out who took him. And then…”


**WE RETRIEVE HIM**


Sarah nodded, the motion small but absolute.


“We’re getting him back.”


The ghost silently agreed.


The volunteers finished loading into the van.


Sarah climbed into the passenger seat and looked one last time at the place where Reeves had vanished.


She whispered—  

Not for any ears but her own:


“Hold on. I’m coming.”


Callen started the engine.


They drove into the night.
