# Chapter 35 — Contact


The dust hadn’t even settled before the first operator moved.


No shout.  

No warning.  

No demand for surrender.


Just a silent, calculated advance.


Sarah’s breath hitched as she slid behind a collapsed support beam.  

The ghost flickered, compressing into a low-profile projection to avoid becoming a target.


Reeves didn’t fall back.


He took one step forward.


The operators fanned out—  

Three on the left,  

Two on the right,  

One centered.


Their formation was surgical.  

Efficient.  

Pre-planned.


These men weren’t here to kill.


They were here to **remove**.


Reeves recognized it instantly.


“Extraction formation,” he muttered.  

“They want the hub intact.”


Sarah grit her teeth.  

“Not happening.”


The ghost’s voice shrank to a tight whisper-band inside both of their ears:


**RECOMMEND ENGAGEMENT AT CLOSE RANGE  

AVOID DISTANCE-BASED PROJECTILE DISRUPTORS  

THEIR ARMOR ABSORBS KINETIC IMPACT  

BUT NOT FORCE REDIRECTION**


Reeves adjusted his stance.


“I figured.”


The lead operator surged forward.


Reeves intercepted with the structural projector—  

A slab of industrial-grade alloy meant for construction alignment,  

Now functioning as a blunt-force shield.


The impact rang like a gong.


Sarah winced; sparks burst outward.  

The operator staggered back two steps, recalibrating.


The others shifted formation.


No shouts.  

No signals.  

No comms chatter.


They moved like a single organism.


Behind Reeves, volunteers scrambled to reinforce the remaining internal barricades.


“Get those supports up!” Sarah barked.  

“If the northwest beam goes, we lose the whole roof!”


Another operator lunged.


Reeves pivoted, using the projector as a deflection plate, redirecting the strike into the concrete floor.  

The shock vibrated up his arm but he didn’t give an inch.


Sarah felt the tension tightening between each attack—  

The rhythm of violence,  

The cadence of a unit trained to overwhelm through precision,  

Not force.


Reeves blocked another blow.


“They’re testing my endurance,” he said through clenched teeth.


“They think it’s limited,” Sarah said.


Reeves didn’t reply, but the set of his jaw answered for him.



Two operators broke off toward the equipment racks.


Sarah sprinted to intercept.


One swung a compact shock baton—  

Nonlethal, but enough to drop her cold.


She ducked under it, grabbed a loose steel pipe from the floor, and cracked it across his wrist.


The baton clattered.  

She kicked it away.


The second operator lunged.


The ghost surged forward with a burst of bright interference—  

Just a flicker—  

Enough to throw off the operator’s depth perception.


Sarah used the moment to slam the pipe into his knee.


He dropped with a grunt.


The ghost dimmed again, retreating.


**CANNOT SUSTAIN VISUAL DISRUPTION LONG  

POWER RESERVES AT 34%  

FOCUSING ON STRUCTURAL MONITORING**


“Do what you can,” Sarah whispered.  

“We’ll handle the rest.”



Reeves locked shields with the lead operator again.


This time their helmets were inches apart.


Sarah heard the low growl in Reeves’ voice—  

Not anger, not fear, but the sound a man makes when he refuses to yield ground.


The operator leaned in, applying pressure.


Reeves didn’t move.


Not an inch.


Sarah saw it then—  

The subtle shift of balance,  

The tightening of his grip,  

The gathering of momentum.


“Reeves—” she started.


Too late.


He drove forward like a battering ram.


The operator lost footing, slammed into the wall, and went still.


For a fraction of a second, the remaining operators hesitated.


Not visibly.


But Reeves felt it.


The momentum pivoted.


Sarah seized it.


“We can push them back!” she shouted.


Reeves didn’t look away from the advancing line.


“They weren’t sent to retreat.”


The ghost’s voice entered, quiet but sharp:


**THEIR OBJECTIVE JUST SHIFTED  

SUB-TARGET LOCK IDENTIFIED  

THEY ARE REFOCUSING ON REEVES**


Sarah’s heart stuttered.


“Why him?”


**UNKNOWN  

BUT THEY ARE ADJUSTING FOR CAPTURE, NOT NEUTRALIZATION**


Reeves stepped forward again, projector raised.


“Let them try.”


The operators surged as one.


The warehouse shook.  

The remaining lights flickered.  

The roof groaned under its own weight.


Sarah braced against the wall as the shadow-crew closed around Reeves in a silent, black tide—
