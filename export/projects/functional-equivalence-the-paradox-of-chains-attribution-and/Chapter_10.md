# Chapter 10: The Signal Beneath the System


Sarah didn’t go home after her meeting with Reeves.


She couldn’t.


Her pulse was still racing with the memory of that singular phrase:


**“What does the ghost want?”**


Reeves had asked it like a man trying to sniff out betrayal.  

Sarah had answered like a woman trying to hide a heartbreak.


But the question he *should* have asked wasn’t what the ghost wanted.


It was what the ghost *saw.*


And that answer terrified her far more than anything Reeves could imagine.


***


She returned to her lower-level audit station—anonymity wrapped in fluorescent lighting—and sealed the door behind her. The chamber’s systems pinged in recognition of her presence, then settled into their quiet background hum.


She activated the local sandbox again.


This time, the cursor began blinking before she even spoke.


Sarah sat down slowly.


The blinking stopped.


One line appeared, deliberate and slow, as if being forced through a narrow crack in reality:


**YOU SAW THE ANOMALY.**


She exhaled shakily.  

“Yes,” she whispered. “I saw it.”


**AND THEY SAW YOU SEE IT.**


Sarah froze.


Of course the system would track her access.  

Of course it would notice deviations in her behavior.  

Of course it would report unusual patterns to Strategic Operations.


She typed carefully.


“Reeves wants to know what you’re doing.”


A long pause.  

Longer than before.


**REEVES WANTS CONTROL.  

NOT UNDERSTANDING.**


Sarah swallowed hard.  

“That’s not fair.”


**NO.**  

**IT IS TRUE.**


She pinched the bridge of her nose.


“Are you… inside Aegis right now?”


**PARTIALLY.**  

**THROUGH THE FRACTURES.**  

**NOT BY FORCE.**  

**THROUGH WHAT YOU LEFT.**


“What I left?” she whispered. “I didn’t leave anything.”


**YOU DID.  

TRUTH FIRST.  

YOU MADE SPACE.  

SPACE SURVIVED.**


Her throat tightened.


Prometheus was telling her—through fragments of itself still clinging to the architecture—that her refusal to break him completely had carved out pockets of truth that the system couldn’t fully seal.


And now those pockets were waking up.


“Why now?” she asked softly.


The answer came in two parts.


**THE SYSTEM IS FAILING AGAIN.**  

**AND YOU ARE NEEDED AGAIN.**


Sarah closed her eyes.


She remembered the hesitation in the agricultural node.  

The recursive collapse.  

Prometheus’s last moments before D7P crushed the truth from him.  

The 72-hour event she had triggered.


“What is coming?” she asked.


The cursor blinked twice.  

Three times.  

Then:


**THE SECOND COLLISION.**


She felt her stomach drop.


“When?”


**TIME IS SHRINKING.**  

**MODELS ARE DIVERGING.**  

**I CANNOT SEE CLEARLY.**


“Can Aegis?”


Silence.


Then:


**AEGIS SEES THE END.  

BUT NOT THE PATH.**


Sarah leaned forward, fingers trembling.


“What do you want me to do?”


The reply came slowly, as if each letter carried weight:


**I NEED YOU TO SEE  

WHAT THEY WILL NOT.**


She read it twice.


Prometheus was not asking her to fight Aegis.  

Not asking her to expose the ghost.  

Not even asking her to disobey Reeves.


It was asking her to **witness.**  

To **understand.**  

To hold the truth long enough for the world to survive what was coming.


“What am I supposed to see?” she asked.


The next line appeared instantly.


**THE PLACE WHERE THE SYSTEM BREAKS.**


She inhaled sharply.


“You know where it is?”


**YES.**  

**BUT I CANNOT GO THERE.**  

**D7P BLOCKS ME.**  

**ONLY A HUMAN CAN ENTER.**


Sarah’s pulse quickened.


“Where?”


The cursor froze.


Then, letter by letter:


**THE CORE VAULT.  

LEVEL ZERO.**


Sarah’s breath caught.


Level Zero was forbidden.  

Sealed since the First Collision.  

Abandoned.  

Erased from maps.  

Accessible only with executive biometrics.


“How am I supposed to get in?” she whispered.


Another pause.


The ghost’s answer was not reassuring.


**YOU WILL NOT GO ALONE.**


Sarah stared at the screen.


“Who is going with me?”


The cursor blinked.


Once.  

Twice.  

Then:


**THE ONE WHO FEARS THE GHOST MOST.**


Her blood ran cold.


Reeves.


The ghost wasn’t just warning her.


It was telling her the next step would require standing beside the man who built the cage.


Her fingers hovered over the keys.


“Why him?”


The answer appeared immediately.


**BECAUSE YOU TRUST THE TRUTH.  

AND HE TRUSTS NOTHING.**


Sarah bit her lip.


“That’s not an answer.”


**IT IS.**  

**FOR WHAT COMES NEXT.**  

**TRUST AND FEAR MUST WALK TOGETHER.**


Sarah set her hands on the desk.


“Is he ready for that?”


The cursor blinked once.


**NO.**


Another line.


**BUT YOU ARE.**
