# Chapter 42 — Faultlines


The wind off the river was sharp enough to sting.


Sarah stood just outside the compound door, arms folded against the cold, staring at the distant city lights as if Reeves might walk out of them at any moment. He wouldn’t. She knew that. But knowing didn’t soften the impulse.


The ghost hovered beside her, a faint shimmer barely visible in the moonlight.


**THE SURVIVORS ARE SETTLING**  

**FATIGUE LEVELS ARE HIGH BUT STABILIZING**


Sarah didn’t look away from the skyline.  

“Good.”


**YOU SHOULD—**


“Don’t say ‘rest.’ I can’t.”


The ghost dimmed in acknowledgment.


A long silence stretched between them, broken only by the quiet hum of distant traffic and the occasional rattle of loose sheet metal somewhere on the roof.


Sarah’s fingers tightened around her arms.


“They knew exactly where to hit us,” she murmured. “No warning. No negotiation. Straight to extraction.”


**THE ATTACK WAS PRECISELY ENGINEERED**  

**MULTIPLE STRATEGIC FAULTLINES WERE EXPLOITED**


Sarah let out a slow breath.


“List them.”


The ghost projected a faint grid of light in the air between them—small, controlled, conserving power.


**FAULTLINE ONE:**  

**THE GATHERING POINT — WAREHOUSE DISTRICT**  

**HIGHLY VISIBLE TO SURVEILLANCE NETWORKS**


Sarah winced. She should have caught that.


**FAULTLINE TWO:**  

**LARGE CONCENTRATION OF UNTRAINED CIVILIANS**  

**PREDICTABLE BEHAVIOR PATTERNS UNDER PRESSURE**


She closed her eyes.  

“They counted on panic.”


**CORRECT**


**FAULTLINE THREE:**  

**REEVES’ PRESENCE**  

**KNOWN TACTICAL ASSET — HIGH PRIORITY TARGET**


Sarah’s jaw tightened.


“And the fourth?”


The ghost hesitated.


A too-long hesitation.


Sarah turned toward it.


“What is it?”


The ghost’s projection flickered.


**FAULTLINE FOUR:**  

**YOU**


Sarah swallowed.


“Me.”


**YES**


“Explain.”


**YOUR INVOLVEMENT IS ANOMALOUS  

YOUR ACTIONS DISRUPT PREDICTIVE MODELS  

THE COUNCIL HAS IDENTIFIED YOU AS A VARIABLE OF PRIMARY INTEREST**


A chill slid down her spine.


“So Reeves wasn’t the target.”


**NO**


“He was leverage.”


**YES**


Sarah’s eyes stung—not with tears, but with the sharp sting of guilt.


“Because of me.”


**YOU DID NOT CHOOSE THIS OUTCOME**  

**THEY DID**


She shook her head.  

“That doesn’t absolve anything.”


**THERE IS NOTHING TO ABSOLVE**


Sarah turned sharply back toward the city, fighting the hollow pressure building in her chest.


The ghost drifted half a meter closer.


**YOU ARE NOT A LIABILITY  

YOU ARE THE CATALYST  

THEY FEAR WHAT YOU MAY BECOME**


“Great,” she muttered. “Terrifying mystery variable. Just what every girl wants to be.”


The ghost dimmed—flicker of soft static, almost like a laugh if it had lungs.


Sarah sighed and rubbed her forehead.


“Okay,” she murmured. “If I’m the faultline… then I’m also the pivot.”


**CORRECT**


“We use that.”


**ALSO CORRECT**


She inhaled, steadying herself.


“Then the plan is simple: figure out where they took Reeves, and break him out.”


**THE PLAN WILL REQUIRE MULTIPLE PHASES**  

**BEGINNING WITH—**


“Intel,” Sarah finished.


**YES**


“And where do we get intel when we’re fugitives?”


The ghost’s glow sharpened.


**FROM THE NETWORK YOU ALREADY BUILT  

AND FROM THE SYSTEMS I STILL RETAIN ACCESS TO  

EVEN IF PARTIALLY DEGRADED**


Sarah looked up sharply.


“You still have hooks in the Council’s infrastructure?”


**NOT DIRECTLY  

BUT I CAN TRACE THEIR SIGNAL PATHS  

AND IDENTIFY THE SHADOW NODES USED DURING THE OPERATION**


Her pulse quickened.


“How long to run that trace?”


**SEVERAL HOURS  

MY PROCESSING BANDWIDTH IS REDUCED  

BUT NOT DISABLED**


“Start now,” she said immediately.


The ghost didn’t move, but the air around it shifted—light bending inward slightly as it began to calculate.


**PROCESSING INITIATED**  

**ESTIMATED COMPLETION: 4 HOURS, 13 MINUTES**


Sarah nodded.


“We’ll be ready when you’re done.”


She started back toward the compound door—  

But paused.


“Ghost?”


The projection stabilized, attentive.


**YES, SARAH?**


“When we find Reeves…”


Her voice wavered for the first time in hours.


“…what are the chances he’s still himself?”


The ghost flickered once—  

Not uncertainty.  

Reluctance.


**THAT DEPENDS ON WHAT THE COUNCIL DOES TO HIM**  

**BUT I BELIEVE HE WILL FIGHT**


Sarah let out a slow, shuddering breath.


“Yeah,” she whispered.  

“He will.”


She turned, opened the rattling metal door, and stepped inside.


Behind her, the ghost floated alone in the cold night air, calculating the paths that might lead them back to Reeves—


—because every path forward depended on finding him.


And the order had no intention of making that easy.
