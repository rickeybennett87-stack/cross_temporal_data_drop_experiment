# Chapter 37 — Breakpoint


The moment the operators surged, the air in the warehouse snapped tight.


Reeves shifted first.


He dropped the ruined projector shield, grabbed the nearest operator by the forearm, and used the man’s own momentum to hurl him into the advancing line. The collision broke their perfect formation for half a heartbeat.


Sarah used it.


She sprinted forward, sliding under a sweeping baton strike and slamming her shoulder into an operator’s ribs. It wasn’t enough to knock him down, but it forced him to stumble—and stumble was all she needed.


The volunteers picked up on it.  

Momentum meant survival.


“Push!” Sarah shouted.  

“Don’t give them space!”


For a moment, the tide slowed.


Just a moment.


Then the operators recalibrated again.


Two moved in perfect sync to grab Reeves by both arms. He drove a knee upward, shattering one of their grip-points, but the second tightened around his wrist with mechanical precision.


Another operator closed in behind him.


Sarah saw the trap forming.


“No—Reeves—!”


Too late.  

The third operator locked his elbow around Reeves’ throat from behind, securing leverage. The tactic wasn’t lethal—it was disabling, controlled, practiced.


They weren’t trying to kill him.  

They were trying to carry him out.


Reeves twisted, bracing his boots against the cracked concrete, dragging all three operators with him. His breath came out in a low snarl as he forced himself upright again.


The ghost flickered beside him, its form glitching from strain.


**REEVES CANNOT HOLD ALL THREE**  

**RECOMMEND STRIKE AT—**


Static ripped through the projection.


Sarah didn’t wait for instructions.


She bolted straight toward the cluster, steel pipe raised like a spear. The nearest operator saw her coming, rotated smoothly, and disarmed her with a single upward strike that sent the pipe skittering across the ground.


Sarah staggered back.


The operator advanced.


Reeves tore free of one grip long enough to block a baton aimed at Sarah’s head. The impact echoed through the warehouse.


“You’re not taking her,” he ground out.


The operator didn’t speak—just adjusted aim and struck again.


Sarah ducked the blow, grabbed his wrist, and wrenched the baton sideways. A sharp crack of electricity hissed out as it discharged into the floor.


The ghost pulsed weakly.


**SARAH—LEFT—NOW**


She moved without looking, trusting the direction, and an operator’s fist sliced through the space her face had been.


The fight spiraled into a tight, brutal knot of motion:


- Reeves turning his whole body into a weapon  

- Sarah striking and slipping away  

- Operators adjusting and counter-adjusting  

- Sparks falling from the ceiling  

- The building groaning under another distant shockwave  


Then everything shifted.


The lead operator uttered a single, low tone—  

Not a word, not even a command.  

Just a signal.


Every operator stopped attacking.


They stepped back half a step, forming a clean arc around Reeves.


Sarah froze.


The ghost dimmed.


Reeves lowered his fists, breathing hard, eyes locked on the semicircle closing around him.


“What now?” Sarah whispered.


The operators answered with action.


All at once, they lunged—not at Reeves, but at the volunteers behind him.


Sarah’s stomach dropped.


“Reeves—!”


He saw it too late.


That was the point.


They couldn’t break him head-on.


So they’d changed tactics.


They were going after everyone else.
