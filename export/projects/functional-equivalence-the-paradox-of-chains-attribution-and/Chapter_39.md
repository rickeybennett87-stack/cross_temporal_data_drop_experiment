# Chapter 39 — Falling Steel


The first steel beam dropped like a guillotine.


Sarah threw herself back, hitting the floor hard as a slab of rusted support crashed where she had been standing. The impact sent a shockwave of dust and splinters rolling across the room.


“Move!” she shouted, though her throat burned and she couldn’t tell if anyone heard her.


The remaining volunteers scrambled deeper into the maintenance corridor. Some crawled. Some limped. One had to be carried by two others. The air filled with the metallic shriek of tearing bolts and the rumbling groan of a building giving up its structural fight.


The ghost’s projection flickered wildly.


**SARAH—THE WESTERN WALL IS FAILING  

YOU MUST EVACUATE—NOW**


“I’m not leaving him!” she yelled back, coughing through dust.


Another beam twisted out of the ceiling and crashed down, this one closer. The ground vibrated under her hands. Somewhere behind her, a generator blew with a deafening pop of white light.


The ghost flickered again, dimmer.


**REEVES IS OUT OF RANGE  

TRACKING SIGNAL LOST  

I CANNOT FOLLOW HIM**


Sarah’s stomach turned cold.


She didn’t let it stop her.


She ran.


She vaulted a fallen table, slid under a hanging pipe, ducked past a burst of sparks. A support column cracked and she sprinted before it could fall. The air was thick with dust—every breath a fight.


The breach where the operators had dragged Reeves was half-collapsed. Wires hung like dead vines. Concrete chunks littered the floor. She forced her way through the narrowing opening, ignoring the scrape of metal against her shoulder.


Outside, the night air slammed into her lungs, cool and sharp.


The operators were already halfway across the lot, black silhouettes moving with terrifying efficiency. Reeves was carried between them like a prisoner of war, his body rigid, barely able to move.


“Reeves!” she shouted.


One of the operators turned—not fully, just enough to track her.


The others didn’t break stride.


Sarah stumbled forward, driven by instinct more than strategy.


But she couldn’t catch them.


They were too fast.  

Too trained.  

Too far.


And she was just one woman, choking on dust, legs shaking, lungs burning.


The ghost materialized beside her—weak, translucent, flickering like a candle in wind.


**SARAH—STOP  

YOU CANNOT ENGAGE THEM ALONE**


She ignored it.


She pushed herself into a sprint.


But her body had limits.


She fell to her knees.


The asphalt tore the skin from her palms.


She forced herself upright again.  

Her legs refused.


The operators loaded Reeves into the back of a nondescript armored van.


One of them paused, as though listening to an unheard voice, then climbed in after him.


The rear doors slammed shut.


The engine roared.


The van peeled away into the night, tires kicking gravel.


Sarah’s voice cracked into nothing.


“Please… don’t take him…”


She dragged herself forward again, reaching out to nothing, reaching toward someone she couldn’t reach.


The van vanished around a corner, swallowed by the dark.


Silence followed—  

Except for the sound of the collapsing warehouse behind her.


The ghost hovered beside her, voice thin as static:


**SARAH…  

WE NEED TO MOVE**


She didn’t reply.


Her eyes stayed fixed on the corner where Reeves had disappeared.


Her hands shook.


Her chest rose and fell in uneven, ragged breaths.


“We have to get him back,” she whispered.


Not a vow.  

Not a declaration.


A fact.


A truth.


The only thing she had left to hold onto as the building behind them began its final slide into ruin.
