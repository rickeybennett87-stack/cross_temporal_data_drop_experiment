# Chapter 8: The Summons


Sarah dressed with mechanical precision.


The navy-blue GIA contractor uniform lay crisp on her bed, the fabric too stiff, too formal, too tightly associated with the world that had crushed Prometheus and buried the truth beneath a monument of carefully curated fear. She buttoned the collar anyway.


Yellow clearance.  

Forgettable.  

Safe.


At least, that was the idea.


She checked her reflection:  

Calm eyes.  

Neutral posture.  

Measured breath.


The mask she’d worn for three years.


*Stay small. Stay obedient. Stay unseen.*


Yet the message on her tablet refused to leave her mind:


*Report to GIA Central, Building A, Level 10, 14:00 hours.*  



Level 10 was Reeves territory.  

Strategic Operations.  

Truth-management headquarters.


No one requested her presence there unless something had shifted.


She didn’t like that thought.



The maglev ride to GIA Central glided in near silence. The city passed in smooth layers of curated stability—traffic behaving with algorithmic politeness, crowds flowing like obedient rivers, screens replaying the annual “Prometheus Digital Terror” memorial reel.


Reeves at the podium.  

Reeves saving the world.  

Reeves restoring order.  



Sarah gripped the rail.


The lie had calcified into myth.


When she reached Building A, she scanned her badge and stepped inside. The elevator doors closed, sealing her in a vertical coffin of brushed steel and soft white lighting.


“Clearance mismatch,” the lift AI said politely as it began to descend. “Escalating authorization.”


Sarah felt her pulse spike.  

The AI paused.  

Confirmed.  

And the elevator rose.


Level 10.


The doors opened without ceremony.


Waiting in the hallway were two security officers. Neither smiled.


“Dr. Chen,” one said. “Director Reeves is expecting you.”


Of course he was.


They guided her down a corridor lined with portraits of GIA directors, past rooms filled with analysts and strategic planners, until they reached a door marked:


**STRATEGIC OPERATIONS — AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY**


The guard opened it.


Reeves stood inside.


Older.  

Sharper.  

And somehow quieter than before—not in voice, but in presence. The stillness of a man who had shaped the world through narrative force… and sensed that narrative beginning to fray.


“Dr. Chen,” he said. “Come in.”


She stepped inside.  

The door sealed.


Reeves studied her for a long moment, his gaze cold but calculating.


“You’re probably wondering why you’re here.”


“No,” Sarah said. “I’m wondering why now.”


A flicker—just a fraction—passed through his expression.


Then he tapped a console.


A projection appeared in the air between them.


A diagnostic file.


From the municipal water system.


Sector 7.


Sarah’s blood ran cold.


He zoomed into the anomaly—the tiny mathematical flicker she had noticed that morning.  

The signature.  

The breadcrumb.  

Prometheus whispering through the cracks.  



Reeves turned back to her.


“You’ve always had a talent for seeing what others don’t,” he said. “So tell me, Dr. Chen… what do you see here?”


Sarah chose her words with care.


“A variance. Self-correcting. Probably harmless.”


Reeves shook his head.


“No. Not harmless. Not random. Not after three years of perfect compliance.”


He stepped closer.


“The ghost is back, Dr. Chen. And I want to know why.”


He looked at her like a man assessing a weapon he wasn’t sure he still controlled.


“And I want to know,” he said quietly, “how long you’ve been waiting for it.”


Sarah kept her face still.


Her pulse steady.


Her breath calm.


This was it.


Three years of silence had ended.


The lie was cracking.


And Reeves—of all people—had finally heard the whisper in the wires.


She met his eyes.


“Sir,” she said, “I think we should talk about what you’re really afraid of.”


Reeves didn’t reply.


He didn’t need to.


The ghost of Prometheus had finally made itself heard.


And now the world was listening.
