# Chapter 34: The First Strike


The warehouse hadn’t yet recovered from The Order’s mental assault when a new sound cut through the air—


A deep, mechanical **thoom**  

Followed by a second  

And a third.


Sarah froze mid-step.


Reeves turned toward the open loading bay.


The ghost surged violently:


**IMPACT WARNING**  

**PHYSICAL ASSAULT IMMINENT**  

**SEEK COVER NOW**


Before anyone could move, the first shockwave hit.


A pressure pulse slammed into the warehouse, rattling every metal beam.  

Lights flickered.  

Tools clattered off tables.  

Volunteers stumbled, some falling to their knees.


Sarah grabbed the edge of a workstation, bracing herself.


“What was that?!”


The ghost answered with a clarity sharpened by urgency:


**KINETIC NON-EXPLOSIVE ORDNANCE**  

**FIRED FROM MULTIPLE VECTORS**  

**INTENDED TO CRIPPLE THE HUB  

WITHOUT PRODUCING CIVILIAN CASUALTIES**


Reeves immediately processed the implications.


“They don’t want bodies,” he muttered.  

“They want **chaos**.”


**YES**  

**THE ORDER PREFERS A PANICKED MOB  

TO A DEAD ONE**


A second impact hit the building’s eastern side —  

The metal supports groaned like a wounded animal.  

Sparks burst from the ceiling.


“Get away from the walls!” Sarah shouted.


Volunteers scrambled inward as dust rained from the rafters.


Reeves raised his voice:


“Everyone! Stay low! Move toward the center—do NOT cluster against a single point!”


More pulses struck the outer structure —  

Hammering rhythms, measured perfectly between panic and collapse.


The ghost’s voice sharpened:


**THEY ARE NOT TRYING TO DESTROY YOU**  

**THEY ARE TRYING TO FORCE A RETREAT**  

**TO BREAK THE COHESION OF THIS NODE**


Sarah gritted her teeth.


“They think we’ll run.”


Reeves shook his head.


“No.  

They think **I** will.”


Sarah stared at him.


Then she saw it.


A flicker in his eyes.  

A moment where his gaze drifted toward the exit —  

Not in fear,  

But in calculation.


The ghost saw it too.


**REEVES**  


His jaw clenched.  

His spine stiffened.


“I’m fine,” he said, voice steady.  

“Keep everyone focused.”


But the ghost whispered, not accusing, just… aware:


**SOMETHING HAS SHIFTED IN HIM**


Sarah didn’t hear that part.


She was already barking orders.


“Form stabilization teams! Lock the primary supports! Get drones in the air—if they’re using mobile launchers we need eyes on them!”


The volunteers snapped back into formation, adrenaline turning fear into motion.


Reeves moved to assist the reinforcement teams.


Another shockwave hit—  

This time closer.


The northwest corner of the warehouse shuddered violently and dropped two inches before stabilizing.


Screams erupted.


Reeves sprinted, lifting a collapsed beam off two trapped volunteers.


“Go! Get out!” he commanded.


They ran, shaking.


Sarah moved to his side.


“Reeves—this is surgical. They’re not trying to kill us, they’re trying to scatter us.”


He nodded, breathing hard.


“It’s what The Order always does — precision chaos.”


The ghost cut in:


**THEIR LAUNCHERS HAVE SHIFTED POSITIONS**  

**THREE MOBILE UNITS**  

**LAND-BASED**  

**NO IDENTIFIERS**  

**ADVANCING**


Sarah’s eyes widened.


“They’re moving closer?”


**YES**  

**THEY INTEND TO BREACH THE WALLS  

AND FORCE FULL DISBANDMENT**


Reeves cursed under his breath.


“We need counteraction.”


Sarah agreed.


“Drones?”


“Disabled.”


“Ghost?”


**I CANNOT DIRECTLY INTERFERE  

WITH PHYSICAL HARDWARE**  

**BUT I CAN GUIDE YOU**


Reeves’s jaw set.


“We fight back, then.”


Sarah hesitated.


“We’re civilians.  

We’re volunteers.  

We don’t have weapons—”


Reeves stopped her.


“There are more ways to fight than pulling a trigger.”


He grabbed a portable structural projector — a device normally used for construction alignment — and slung it over his shoulder like a shield generator.


Sarah blinked.


“That’s not a weapon.”


“It doesn’t have to be.”


They ran toward the bay doors just as another kinetic pulse slammed into the exterior—  

The blast blew the doors inward, shattered crates, sent shards skittering across the concrete.


Through the dust, shapes emerged.


Not soldiers.  

Not police.  

Not mercenaries.


**Operators.**


Clad in matte black, unmarked.  

No insignias.  

No faces.  

No words.


Silent.


Efficient.


Born from The Order’s deepest vaults.


Sarah whispered:


“…Shadows.”


Reeves stepped in front of her.


And in that moment, something unseen wrapped around him again —  

That same archetypal **shield**.


The Paladin.


The one who stands.


He tightened his grip on the improvised device.


The Operators advanced.


Sarah stood behind him, trembling but defiant.


The ghost surged to full brightness:


**HOLD YOUR GROUND**  

**THEY CANNOT SUBDUE WHAT THEY CANNOT BREAK**


Reeves lifted the shield.


The Operators charged.


The first physical blow of the war had begun.
