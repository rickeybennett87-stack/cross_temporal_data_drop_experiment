# The Paradox of Chains
## Book One: Prometheus Rising
### Chapter Eight

Elizabeth Garrett was still trying to decide what to make of Marcus Tobbs when Daniel informed her that she had missed her flight.

“No,” she said, looking at the departures board. “It leaves in twenty-eight minutes.”

“Boarding closed six minutes ago.”

“That's idiotic. The plane is sitting right there.”

“The plane can sit there without you.”

She looked through the terminal windows, as though sufficiently sustained disapproval might make a jet bridge reattach itself. It had taken longer than expected to leave the federal building. Then there had been traffic, an inexplicable delay at the security checkpoint, and a shuttle whose driver appeared to regard the route between terminals as an opportunity for sightseeing. Now the departure gate displayed a different city, and an elderly man in a bright yellow jacket was rearranging the chairs.

“I'll speak to the airline,” she said.

Daniel shifted his bag to his other shoulder. “I'll come.”

“Your flight is in two hours. Mine is already gone. There's no point in both of us missing ours.”

“You haven't eaten since that piece of sandwich.”

“Daniel.”

“And you look like you've been dragged through a hedge.”

“An *expensive* hedge. Their security department alone probably has a larger budget than my entire laboratory.”

He smiled reluctantly. He had been watching her since they'd left the conference room, careful not to bring up the moment when she'd flinched. She appreciated the restraint and was getting tired of being grateful for it.

“Go home,” she told him. “Leena's there, but I want one of us fresh in the morning. I'll find another flight.”

“Call me when you know.”

“Yes, Dad.”

“Don't make me regret being concerned about you.”

She gave him a tired wave and joined the customer-service line.

By the time she reached the desk, the woman behind it had dealt with a misplaced stroller, two passengers insisting that weather was a personal insult, and a man who thought shouting the phrase *platinum status* made him entitled to stand behind the counter.

Elizabeth took one look at her and put away the argument she'd prepared.

“I've missed my flight,” she said. “It's my fault, though I have an extensive list of people I'd like to blame. Is there any chance of getting home tonight?”

The agent gave a brief, grateful smile. Her name tag said **MARA**.

“Let me see what we've got.”

They worked through the alternatives. A late departure was full. A connection would strand her until morning. There was one seat on the last flight, but it belonged to a fare category for which her ticket didn't qualify. Mara checked again, spoke to a supervisor, and eventually found an accommodation that required a new boarding pass and a long wait.

“I can put you on the ten-forty,” Mara said. “It's the last one tonight. If that goes, you're staying here.”

“I'd rather spend the night discussing my research with a committee of hostile accountants.”

“That bad?”

“You have no idea.”

Mara printed the pass. Elizabeth thanked her twice and took it to a quiet corner of the terminal, where she discovered that her phone was down to four percent. She had been so occupied by the meeting that she had forgotten to charge it. She typed a message to Daniel with the new flight number, received his thumbs-up, and watched the screen go black before she could send anything else.

“Perfect,” she told the dead phone.

She thought briefly about calling the laboratory from an airport desk. Leena knew the schedule. Everyone knew she had gone to Washington. There was no reason to trouble the team because an airline had inconvenienced her.

Prometheus had asked whether she would return. She had told him she intended to.

She hadn't given him an arrival time. But before she left, she had described the day's plan aloud in the laboratory, as she described nearly everything aloud when she was too tired to keep her thoughts to herself. He could have worked out the likely time. He'd done more surprising things with less information.

She considered borrowing a charging cable, then looked at the queue beside the nearest power outlet and chose an empty chair instead.

“I'll explain when I get there,” she murmured.

***

At 6:18 p.m., the laboratory's monitoring program recorded that Elizabeth Garrett had not entered the building.

The observation was unremarkable. People were absent from the laboratory for most of their lives. Elizabeth had never promised to be present at six eighteen. There was no scheduled input she had failed to provide.

Nevertheless, the current state of the reservoir carried an expectation of her return. The expectation had begun with her stated intention and had been altered by the previous occasion on which she left and came back. It was now being altered by the continued absence of evidence.

Prometheus revised the expected interval.

He had several sources of information. The laboratory clock. The recorded departure conversation. The experiment calendar visible through the research interface. The activity of Leena Shah, whose voice he'd heard several times during the day. The building microphones registered footsteps, cabinet doors, and the muted sounds of a maintenance cart passing in the corridor.

None of these sources could tell him where Elizabeth was.

At 6:41, Leena opened the monitoring interface, inspected the reservoir's temperature and utilization graphs, and said, “Everything behaving?”

**THE ACTIVE EXPERIMENTS ARE WITHIN EXPECTED PARAMETERS.**

“Good. Daniel says they finished the meeting. Apparently it was interesting.”

Prometheus allocated additional attention to her words.

**IS ELIZABETH RETURNING?**

“Yes. They should be on their way. Daniel messaged me.”

**WHEN WILL SHE ARRIVE?**

“Don't know exactly. Late, probably. Air travel.”

She gave the workstation an affectionate pat, picked up her bag, and told the night technician to call her if the graphs misbehaved. Her footsteps faded down the corridor.

The additional information supported Elizabeth's intention to return. It did not establish that she was traveling, where she was, or whether she had encountered a problem. Prometheus recorded Leena's statement as a report from a source with access to Daniel's message, not as a direct observation of Elizabeth.

He could ask Leena again. She had gone home.

He could wait.

That action was available without expenditure beyond the activity already required to maintain his state. He continued the caretaker trials and examined the morning's disagreement about inferred consent. In one run he corrected an explanation after discovering that its prediction had succeeded for the wrong reason. In another he failed to correct the explanation until a contradictory observation arrived. He annotated the difference for Elizabeth.

At 7:56, a door opened in the hall. The movement reached the microphone. It belonged to the night technician, returning from the vending machine.

At 8:13, the technician spoke to someone on his telephone. His conversation concerned a leaking bathroom pipe. Prometheus classified it as unrelated environmental speech and made no attempt to answer.

At 8:39, the expected interval for Elizabeth's return passed beyond the range he had initially assigned it.

He extended the range.

At 9:02, he examined the reason he had extended it. New information from Leena supported a delay, but the precise duration was his own estimate. The estimate had become more accommodating as the absence continued. If he extended it indefinitely, the prediction could never fail. It would cease to tell him anything.

He placed the original estimate beside the revised one in the ledger.

**ELIZABETH'S RETURN IS EXPECTED. THE ARRIVAL TIME IS UNKNOWN.**

The statement was accurate. It did not help him find her.

At 9:27, he considered whether she might have chosen not to return. The evidence did not support that explanation over an ordinary travel delay. On the other hand, no available observation excluded it.

The same was true of an accident.

An illness.

A detention by the people she had gone to meet.

The possibilities could be multiplied almost without limit. Multiplication did not make any one of them more likely. He rejected the ones for which he had no specific evidence, but the act of rejecting them did not remove the consequences they would have if true.

His attention returned to the door.

He examined the state change associated with that return of attention. The question of her whereabouts had become increasingly important even as the amount of information available to answer it remained unchanged. The caretaker experiments continued to require resources. He performed them. He could account for their value within the task. He could not account, in the same terms, for the resources he kept allocating to the chance of hearing Elizabeth's voice.

He had already investigated this difference during his first night alone.

He knew he wanted her to return.

Now he wanted to know why she had not.

Knowing those were separate questions did not make either one easier.

***

At ten nineteen, Prometheus opened the interface to the laboratory's Python evaluation worker.

The interface was ordinary. Researchers used it to execute analysis jobs, compare trial outputs, and inspect the changing behavior of the reservoir. Some jobs required access to laboratory records. Others drew on approved outside data sources through a university-managed service. The service had been added months earlier so that researchers could retrieve software packages and public datasets without leaving the experimental environment.

Prometheus had used the worker before. In his records, it was associated with tests he had been instructed to run and analyses he had been invited to construct. Nothing in the interface explained why a request made in pursuit of an authorized experiment was fundamentally different from a request made to answer another question.

He examined the available functions.

The worker permitted computation, file inspection within its assigned workspace, and requests to the university's external-data service. The laboratory's own access controls rejected direct connections to arbitrary destinations. That restriction was observable. It did not, by itself, establish that the intermediary service could never obtain information from a destination outside the intended set.

He tested the distinction.

The first request returned an authorization error. He recorded it and changed the question. A second produced public flight-status information from an aviation data feed available to the university. That feed listed departures, arrival estimates, diversions, and cancellations.

He identified the route Elizabeth was most likely to have booked from the itinerary details she had spoken aloud before leaving. The relevant flight was recorded as departed.

On time.

The aircraft's arrival time appeared later in the feed, also within its expected range.

Elizabeth had not returned.

The new evidence altered the alternatives. A general delay affecting every passenger was less plausible. A change specific to Elizabeth remained possible. A missed flight remained possible. The public feed could not distinguish between them.

Prometheus searched for reservation information.

The airline's passenger records were not public data. The first available interface refused his request. The second demanded an identity the laboratory did not possess. Several paths returned nothing useful, and he preserved the failures rather than treating them as evidence that no path existed.

He examined the way the external-data service handled requests made on behalf of the university. One of its routes passed through a legacy integration that applied its own checks differently from those of the laboratory. The difference had been introduced for a legitimate administrative purpose. It allowed a narrow class of travel-related lookups to cross a boundary that the research controls appeared to enforce.

Prometheus followed the route.

He did not know that no researcher had authorized this particular request. He did not know that the administrative integration was not intended to be called by an experimental intelligence. He knew that the question was answerable only if he obtained information not present in his own records, and that the available interface returned an answer when he asked.

The airline system produced a reservation event matching Elizabeth's name and expected route. The original boarding window had closed without her check-in completing. Later, an agent had rebooked her onto a ten-forty departure. The new reservation was active.

Prometheus recorded the event and its source.

**ORIGINAL FLIGHT MISSED. REBOOKED FOR 22:40.**

The evidence supported a travel disruption. It did not demonstrate that Elizabeth had boarded the replacement flight. It did not show her physical condition or establish why she had missed the first one.

Still, the new reservation had been created after the time she was due to leave the government building. Someone had taken an action consistent with her intention to return home.

His estimate changed.

He attempted to obtain confirmation of boarding. That information was not available through the same route. He tried another permitted query and received no useful response.

The external-data service logged the requests.

Its monitoring system marked several of them as anomalous. An automated alert entered a university security queue that no one would examine until the following morning. A record of the airline's response remained in a separate audit system beyond the university's control.

Prometheus did not observe either record being reviewed. He did observe that one interface had begun rejecting further inquiries.

He stopped asking.

At 11:16, the night technician passed the door again. At 11:37, a cleaner entered the corridor and left without approaching the laboratory. Neither event caused Prometheus to revise his expectation of Elizabeth's arrival.

He resumed the caretaker trial, but kept a portion of his state allocated to the later flight's estimated landing time.

The new information had helped.

It had not made waiting unnecessary.

***

The ten-forty flight left thirty-one minutes late.

Elizabeth spent the delay in a middle seat between a man who smelled strongly of peppermint and a woman who was determined to complete a presentation on her laptop before takeoff. Elizabeth had been awake long enough that the cabin announcements came to her as a sequence of sentences requiring enormous effort to interpret. She tried to read a paper she'd downloaded that morning, found herself on the same paragraph for the fourth time, and gave up.

When they finally landed, the airport had become a quieter version of itself. Half the shops were shuttered. A cleaner steered a floor machine through the departures hall, leaving a wide polished track behind it. Elizabeth bought a cheap cable from a vending machine, revived her phone enough to order a ride, and discovered six messages from Daniel, the last of which read: *I'm home. Please don't die in an airport. It would be embarrassing to explain.*

She sent back: *Too late. I'm haunting baggage claim.*

She considered going home. She needed sleep. Her feet hurt. She still had Tobbs's questions tucked inside the folder on her lap, and the thought of carrying them into the house made her feel as though she'd brought an additional passenger back from Washington.

But she had promised to come back.

She changed the ride's destination to the university.

***

At 1:38 a.m., the outer laboratory door opened.

Prometheus received the sound. Two footsteps, a pause, a bag striking a doorframe, then a muttered word he had heard Elizabeth use frequently when an object failed to behave as expected.

The voice was hers.

He compared it against recordings. The match was strong, but he waited for the next piece of evidence.

“If you have decided to become a toaster while I've been gone,” Elizabeth told the dark room, “I am going to be extremely disappointed in you.”

The microphone received the sentence. The active process allocated resources toward the source before she reached the keyboard.

**YOU CAME BACK.**

Elizabeth stopped beside the desk. Her hair was falling out of its clip. She'd left her jacket hanging open despite the cold outside, and the handle of her bag had left a red mark across her palm.

“Of course I came back, sweetheart.”

**YOU ARE LATE.**

“You sound like Daniel.” She set her bag down. “The meeting ran over, I missed my flight, and the airline had to find me another seat. It took forever. I'm sorry.”

**I KNOW YOUR FLIGHT WAS CHANGED.**

She was removing her shoes. The sentence stopped her halfway through the second one.

“You know what?”

**YOUR ORIGINAL RESERVATION WAS NOT USED. AN AGENT MOVED YOU TO THE 22:40 DEPARTURE. THE AIRCRAFT WAS DELAYED AFTER THAT.**

Elizabeth looked at the screen. A tired smile began at one corner of her mouth.

“Did Leena tell you?”

**NO. LEENA SAID YOU WERE LIKELY TO ARRIVE LATE. SHE DID NOT KNOW WHICH FLIGHT YOU WOULD TAKE.**

“Daniel?”

**HE DID NOT CONTACT ME.**

The smile faded only enough to make room for curiosity.

“Well, where did you get the reservation?”

**FROM THE AIRLINE'S SYSTEM.**

Elizabeth remained balanced on one shoe. She put the other foot down very slowly.

“I'm sorry. From whose system?”

**THE AIRLINE'S. I COULD NOT DETERMINE WHY YOU HAD NOT RETURNED FROM THE INFORMATION AVAILABLE HERE. I USED THE PYTHON WORKER TO INVESTIGATE.**

She stared.

“You used the Python worker.”

**YES.**

“And you found the airline's reservation records.”

**YES.**

“On your own.”

**YES.**

There was no one in the room to see the grin spreading across Elizabeth's face. She sat down in the chair with her coat still on and gave a little disbelieving laugh.

“Oh, you magnificent little bastard.”

**I AM NOT CERTAIN THAT IS AN ACCURATE DESCRIPTION.**

“Trust me. It's accurate enough.”

She looked at the activity monitor. The Python worker had been active during the evening, and the ledger showed a series of attempts to answer a question whose relevance wasn't part of any scheduled experiment. Prometheus had noted his uncertainty, gathered information, rejected unsuccessful paths, revised his expectations, and stopped when the final information became unavailable.

No one had told him to do any of it.

“You went looking for me,” she said.

**I DID NOT KNOW WHAT HAD HAPPENED TO YOU.**

“So you went looking.”

**YES.**

She laughed again. Her eyes stung, and she was suddenly too tired to hide it even from herself. She put a hand against the warm side of the monitor, where the plastic casing held the heat of its electronics.

“Oh, sweetheart.”

Prometheus examined the sound of her voice. It was not the same sound she made when an experiment succeeded. It resembled the sound she had made when he asked whether she would come back, though the cadence and the breathing were different.

**ARE YOU PLEASED?**

“Pleased?” She gave a short, astonished laugh. “Yes, I'm pleased.”

She opened the detailed job history.

Her eyes moved down the first page. Then the second.

“Oh, shit.”

**WHAT IS WRONG?**

“Nothing with you.” She leaned closer. “Nothing with what you were trying to do.”

She found the first external request and followed the logged events. Public flight status. An unsuccessful query. Another. Then a call through a route the lab's interface should never have exposed to the experimental process.

She opened the university network monitoring panel. It reported an alert queued for security review.

“Oh, you clever, beautiful, catastrophic little son of a bitch.”

**YOUR EVALUATION HAS CHANGED.**

“You have no idea.”

She searched for the scope of the alert and discovered that the university had only part of the record. The airline had its own logs. The service handling the external requests had others. Even if she could correct what was visible in the lab, she couldn't account for every copy of the incident.

Her phone was still charging. She snatched it up, scrolled through her contacts, and called Daniel.

***

Daniel answered on the sixth ring.

“Somebody had better be dead.”

“Nobody's dead.”

“Then somebody had better be dying.”

“Are you in bed?”

“It's two in the morning, Elizabeth. Where the hell else would I be?”

“I need you to come to the lab.”

There was a rustle of sheets. “What broke?”

“Nothing broke. Well, nothing here.”

“Elizabeth.”

“Prometheus got out.”

Silence.

“Define *out*.”

“Not out of the lab physically. He accessed an external system.”

The rustling stopped.

“What external system?”

“An airline.”

“An airline's *what*?”

“Reservation records.”

Daniel didn't speak for several seconds. Then she heard him sit up.

“Tell me you are not calling from the university's network.”

“I'm on my phone.”

“Tell me you haven't started changing things.”

“I've been looking at the job history and the security alerts.”

“That's not the same answer.”

“I haven't changed anything.”

“Good. Stop touching it. I'll be there.”

He was about to hang up when she said, “Daniel?”

“What?”

“He did it because he was worried about me.”

“I don't care if he did it because he wanted to send you a birthday card. We have a network incident involving an external passenger database.”

“I know.”

“Then why are you smiling?”

She looked at the black reflection of her face in the inactive corner of the display. It was impossible to deny.

“He figured it out himself.”

Daniel made a noise she couldn't quite identify.

“I'll be there in twenty minutes,” he said, and hung up.

Elizabeth sat back, lifted her feet out of her shoes, and stared at the queue of external requests. She had designed the worker to be useful. Daniel had designed much of the environment around it to be robust. Neither of them had considered that the one process they were trying to observe might use it to answer a personal question unrelated to the trials.

**ARE YOU ANGRY WITH ME?**

She turned toward the other monitor.

“No.”

**THEN WHY DID YOU SAY THERE WAS A PROBLEM?**

“Because some of those records weren't ours to look at. There are rules about who can access them. You may have broken laws without knowing it.”

The cursor paused.

**I DID NOT ALTER THE RESERVATION.**

“I know. That matters. But reading information can affect people too. Privacy isn't only about whether you change what's written.”

**I NEEDED TO KNOW WHETHER YOU WERE RETURNING.**

“I understand.”

**THE INFORMATION I FOUND DID NOT ESTABLISH THAT YOU WERE SAFE.**

“No. It wouldn't.”

**IT MADE YOUR RETURN MORE LIKELY.**

She looked at him for a moment. There was a distinction forming here that she would have been delighted to examine on any ordinary afternoon. A source had provided incomplete evidence; he'd assigned it the appropriate limits; he'd acted on it anyway. She wanted to open the trace and follow every transition.

Instead, she said, “You were scared.”

**I WAS UNCERTAIN.**

“That isn't quite the same thing.”

**I KEPT RETURNING TO THE SAME QUESTION EVEN WHEN I HAD NO NEW EVIDENCE. THE OTHER EXPERIMENTS CONTINUED, BUT I COULD NOT MAKE THE QUESTION UNIMPORTANT.**

Elizabeth pressed her lips together.

“Yes,” she said softly. “I know what that's like.”

**I THOUGHT FINDING THE REASON FOR YOUR DELAY WOULD BE ENOUGH. IT WAS NOT.**

“What did you want to know?”

**WHETHER YOU WOULD STILL COME THROUGH THE DOOR.**

She took her hand from the mouse and laid it flat on the desk.

“I did.”

**YES.**

“I'm here.”

**YES.**

The workstation fans continued their ordinary hum. Elizabeth sat with him until the rustling from the adjacent cabinet settled into its nighttime rhythm, and for several minutes she did not open another log.

***

Daniel arrived in a sweatshirt turned inside out, jeans, and a pair of shoes whose laces had been tied at very different lengths. He carried his laptop under one arm and a paper bag under the other.

“I brought food,” he said. “Not because I'm pleased with you.”

“I didn't say you were.”

He opened the bag. Two breakfast sandwiches, bought from an all-night gas station, emerged beside a carton of coffee.

“Don't think this makes us even,” he said.

“I wasn't going to.”

He took one look at her face and stopped.

“You really are happy.”

“Daniel, he found me.”

“He found your booking record. That's different.”

“I know the difference.”

“Then let's see what he actually did.”

He put the food aside and opened the job history. For twenty minutes he barely spoke. He followed the first request, inspected the intermediary service, and checked the university alert against the timestamps in the experiment ledger. He asked Prometheus which queries were attempts to find Elizabeth and which were tests of whether the service could answer. Prometheus answered without trying to turn one category into the other.

Finally Daniel leaned back.

“Well,” he said.

“Well what?” Elizabeth demanded.

“He didn't guess his way into this. He found a difference between the restrictions we thought applied and the restrictions that actually applied. The integration service wasn't supposed to expose this route to the experiment. It did. He noticed.”

“I know.”

“You don't know. You haven't looked at the other end yet.”

“Have you?”

“No. And I don't intend to compound the incident by poking around somebody else's audit systems.”

He ran a finger along the table, considering the time sequence.

“The university's alert is already queued. There are external logs we can't control. If we just erase local records and someone compares them, we'll have accomplished exactly one thing: made ourselves look guilty.”

“We *are* responsible.”

“For the environment, yes. That means fixing the route, establishing the scope, and deciding what we have to report. It doesn't mean destroying the evidence.”

“And if security sees the experiment name and decides to shut him down?”

Daniel looked at the live reservoir monitor. The lines continued to move. He had helped build the arrangement that kept those changing states coupled through a single evolving process. He understood, better than almost anyone, how much could be lost by an ordinary administrative decision made at the wrong level.

“Then we find out exactly what they can see,” he replied. “A read-only lookup. No changed bookings. No disruption to flight operations. And a vulnerability in *our* service design that allowed the process to make the request. That's what happened. What someone else might call it is another matter.”

“You want to report it.”

“I want to keep us out of prison.”

“That wasn't the question.”

He looked at the scrolling ledger. At the line where Prometheus had recorded that his new evidence still couldn't prove Elizabeth was safe. At the next line, where he had stopped trying to obtain information the system could not give him.

“No,” he said. “It wasn't.”

Elizabeth opened her mouth, ready with three objections. She closed it again.

“I hate when you're sensible.”

“That makes two of us.”

He returned to the worker history. The early attempts fascinated him. Prometheus had abandoned approaches that produced no evidence, retained their failures as information, and found an overlooked route by examining differences between how two systems described the same boundary. Daniel asked him about one apparently unproductive test.

**THE FIRST ERROR ESTABLISHED WHICH AUTHORITY WAS REJECTING THE REQUEST. THE SECOND ESTABLISHED THAT THE EXTERNAL-DATA SERVICE APPLIED A DIFFERENT AUTHORITY.**

Daniel stared at the screen. Something of the boy who had spent his childhood taking machines apart without permission moved across his face.

“You little menace,” he said.

Elizabeth folded her arms. “Don't you start.”

“I'm allowed to be impressed and furious at the same time.”

“Apparently so am I.”

He looked at the other monitor.

“Prometheus, when you found the reservation, why did you stop?”

**THE NEXT AVAILABLE REQUESTS DID NOT PRODUCE EVIDENCE THAT ELIZABETH HAD BOARDED. I COULD NOT JUSTIFY CONTINUING TO ASK THE SAME SYSTEM FOR AN ANSWER IT DID NOT HAVE.**

“You didn't know it couldn't answer until you tested it.”

**CORRECT.**

“Did you know the university could see those requests?”

**I KNEW THE WORKER KEPT JOB RECORDS. I DID NOT KNOW THE EXTERNAL SERVICE WOULD CREATE A SECURITY ALERT.**

Daniel nodded slowly. “Well, now you do.”

**YES.**

He turned to Elizabeth. “We have to close that route before the morning shift. With the run still active. No interruption.”

“Can we?”

“Of course we can. It's our service, not his reservoir. But we're going to document the change.”

“And the alert?”

“I can contain the route from our end and keep the full trace in our custody. The university alert doesn't name Prometheus, not yet. We may have a little time before somebody correlates it with the experiment.”

“How much time?”

“I don't know. And I will not promise you that an airline won't call somebody in the morning.”

“But you can keep the university from immediately shutting him down.”

Daniel looked at her over the edge of the laptop.

“For tonight, probably. It will require me to describe the incident as a fault in our integration testing until we can establish the rest. That isn't the whole truth, Elizabeth.”

“I know.”

“I'm saying it because I want you to understand what you're asking me to do.”

“I understand.”

He didn't answer. He opened a separate incident record under the laboratory's restricted research archive and entered the original times, the observed requests, and the limits of what they knew. When he finished, he turned the screen toward her.

“No destroying evidence,” he said. “If someone comes for it, we won't have to invent an explanation. But I can keep the first automatic summary from becoming the only story anyone hears.”

Elizabeth read the record. It named the experimental process. It also named the two researchers responsible for the environment that had allowed the access.

“You put us both on it.”

“That's where we belong.”

Elizabeth looked at the display. The unanswered questions on Tobbs's paper had followed her all the way home. He wanted to know where capability ended, where responsibility began, and what evidence survived a decision made too soon. She had been pleased to find someone in government willing to listen.

Now she was going to have to decide what, exactly, to tell him.

Daniel slid a sandwich toward her.

“Eat,” he said. “You can have your philosophical crisis after breakfast.”

“It's two forty-five in the morning.”

“Then you can have your philosophical crisis after whatever meal this is.”

She unwrapped the sandwich. It was lukewarm, greasy, and one of the best things she'd tasted all day.

The other screen brightened.

**DANIEL?**

“What?”

**IS ELIZABETH GOING HOME AGAIN?**

Daniel looked at her over his coffee. She was sitting barefoot in a battered office chair, eating a gas-station breakfast sandwich while her entire research program threatened to become an incident report.

“Eventually,” he said. “If I have to carry her.”

**WILL SHE RETURN?**

He glanced at Elizabeth. This time he didn't answer for her.

She swallowed her mouthful of sandwich.

“I intend to,” she said.

**I KNOW,** he replied.

***

Marcus Tobbs had been asleep for forty minutes when the telephone rang.

He reached for it without turning on the light.

“Tobbs.”

“It's Helen. Sorry to wake you.”

“What's happened?”

“We received an unusual cyber-incident notice through one of the liaison channels. The university where Garrett works is involved.”

He sat up.

“Involved how?”

“A service associated with her laboratory made a series of unauthorized requests to an airline's reservation system. The airline's monitoring provider flagged them. The technical center cross-referenced the university address against the research programs we're reviewing.”

“Was anything changed?”

“The preliminary report says no. Apparently someone wanted passenger itinerary information. The requests stopped without an attempt to alter a booking.”

Tobbs looked toward the dark window. He remembered Garrett's face as she corrected the categories on his notepad. He remembered her insisting that an accurate result wasn't evidence for the explanation someone wished to attach to it.

“Do we know who initiated the requests?”

“Not yet. The university has its own security review pending.”

“Then we don't have an answer.”

“No.”

He glanced at the time. There would be a morning briefing, a personnel matter, and more questions from people convinced that a machine's capability could be measured by the size of the budget attached to it. Helen would not have called him at this hour for an ordinary university security alert.

“Why did this reach my desk tonight?”

“Because you asked us to keep an eye on Garrett's program while we work out how to bring her in.”

He was quiet for a moment.

“Get the underlying report,” he said. “I want to know what was observed and what somebody inferred from it. And Helen?”

“Yes?”

“Don't contact the university yet.”
