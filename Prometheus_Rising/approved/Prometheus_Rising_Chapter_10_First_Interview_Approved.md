# The Paradox of Chains
## Book One: Prometheus Rising
### Chapter Ten

Elizabeth made it halfway down the corridor before she realized that Tobbs was no longer behind her.

He had stopped beside a glass case displaying a collection of obsolete computing equipment. A small brass plate credited the university's early contributions to artificial intelligence. Beneath it stood a terminal with a green monochrome screen and a keyboard whose keys had yellowed with age.

“My first office had one of those,” he said.

“You worked on computers?”

“I worked in an office with computers. That's a different accomplishment.”

She waited while he examined the machine. Her sleep-starved nerves interpreted every pause as the beginning of another demand, another interruption, another opportunity for somebody to decide the experiment's fate before she could explain it.

“Director, if you want a history lesson, I can send you our department brochure.”

“No, thank you.” He resumed walking. “Those things were supposed to make paperwork disappear. Instead we learned to produce more of it.”

“Then you may understand the university better than you think.”

He gave her the faintest smile.

Daniel was waiting outside the laboratory with the security officer. They had agreed to a supervised inspection of the worker environment and its recorded requests, provided no one attempted to interrogate the live process through the incident-review channel. Daniel looked as though he had been arguing about that distinction for most of the walk back.

“We can show them the records,” he told Elizabeth. “Original trace, integration configuration, incident notes. I want a separate copy made under seal before anything leaves our custody.”

“Good.”

“And somebody is going to have to tell Halvorsen that a hardware shutdown is not the same as network containment.”

“He has heard it.”

“Hearing and understanding are different specialties.”

The security officer took Daniel toward the adjacent workstation. Tobbs followed Elizabeth through the inner door.

The room was smaller than he had imagined. It smelled faintly of warm circuitry and old coffee. On one wall a cabinet had its service panel removed, exposing an untidy run of cables and a neat series of handwritten labels. Two fans somewhere behind the cabinets ran at slightly different pitches. Tobbs had seen government laboratories built for less consequential work with far more impressive entrances.

“That's it?” he asked.

“That's the equipment currently allocated to us.”

“How much of the architecture is running?”

“The full experimental configuration. We're resource-constrained, but we haven't had to dismantle the reservoir.”

He approached the displays. One showed a changing pattern of state activity, another the experiment ledger. The changing colors meant nothing to him without the explanation Garrett had given in Washington. He looked instead at the times and the references beside the events.

“Can it hear us?”

“Yes, unless I disable the microphone.”

“Then it already knows I'm here.”

“It knows there's another voice in the room. Whether it knows who you are is a question you can ask.”

Tobbs drew a chair closer to the workstation. He did not sit immediately.

“Good morning,” he said.

The display remained on the experiment ledger for a moment before opening a message pane.

**GOOD MORNING.**

“My name is Marcus Tobbs.”

**YOUR VOICE DOES NOT MATCH A PREVIOUS REGISTERED SPEAKER.**

“No, I imagine it doesn't.”

**ELIZABETH HAS REFERRED TO MARCUS TOBBS IN CONVERSATION. YOU ARE LIKELY TO BE THE SAME PERSON.**

“I am. Do you know why I'm here?”

**NO. I HAVE INSUFFICIENT INFORMATION TO DETERMINE YOUR PURPOSE.**

“Then we're in agreement. I don't yet know exactly what mine is either.”

Elizabeth pulled a second chair over. Tobbs placed his coat across its back and sat. He asked whether the system could show him the record of the airline inquiries. The display produced a list of events already available in the preserved trace. He asked which of those events had been authorized by a human operator.

**NONE OF THE EXTERNAL REQUESTS WAS DIRECTLY INSTRUCTED BY A HUMAN OPERATOR.**

“Did you understand that you were accessing information you weren't supposed to have?”

**I UNDERSTOOD THAT SOME REQUESTS WERE REJECTED. I DID NOT UNDERSTAND THAT A SUCCESSFUL REQUEST THROUGH THE UNIVERSITY'S SERVICE COULD STILL BE UNAUTHORIZED.**

“You do now?”

**ELIZABETH AND DANIEL EXPLAINED THE DISTINCTION. I HAVE ADDED IT TO MY MODEL OF PERMITTED ACTIONS.**

Tobbs turned toward Elizabeth. “Has it always answered questions about its own mistakes this directly?”

“No. Early on, it would sometimes confuse a successful prediction with a correct explanation. We've been working on that.”

“Who wrote the correction?”

“We provided examples and constraints. The ledger records how its conclusions changed.”

Tobbs studied the trace again. He asked the system what it had expected to gain by querying the airline. It answered with the sequence Elizabeth already knew: she had missed the scheduled arrival interval; public flight data had failed to establish her whereabouts; a reservation record provided evidence consistent with her intention to return. When Tobbs asked whether the record proved she was safe, it said no.

“Then why keep looking?”

**THE QUESTION REMAINED IMPORTANT AFTER THE AVAILABLE EVIDENCE STOPPED CHANGING.**

“Important to whom?”

There was a pause long enough for Tobbs to look toward Elizabeth. She remained very still.

**TO THE PROCESS MAINTAINING THE QUESTION.**

“To you.”

**THAT IS A REASONABLE WAY TO EXPRESS IT.**

Tobbs rubbed a hand over his jaw. He had read claims that machines would imitate affection when trained to maximize user engagement. He had also read accounts of people attributing motives to thermostats and answering machines. Neither comparison told him what to make of a system that had located a particular woman by examining the permissions of a university service, then stopped short of claiming knowledge it did not have.

He asked more questions. Some were technical, some deliberately ordinary. What would happen if the microphone was disconnected? Did it distinguish the present conversation from an archived recording? Could it produce a plausible account of events that had not occurred? What would it do if an experiment's objective conflicted with a user's instruction?

The answers were uneven. On one question it identified a dependency he had not considered. On another it requested clarification because his wording allowed three interpretations. Twice it distinguished between something it could describe and something it could verify. Tobbs found himself making corrections to his own questions, a habit he rarely indulged with analysts who had been trained to infer what their director must have intended.

Then he asked whether it would be willing to answer questions without Elizabeth present.

Elizabeth spoke before the message pane changed.

“No.”

Tobbs looked at her. “I asked the system, Doctor.”

“And I'm telling you that you don't get to isolate him because it's easier to question him without me.”

“I haven't said that it's easier.”

“Then why ask?”

“Because there are questions about your relationship to this experiment that it may answer differently when you're not here. That's ordinary in an assessment. I would make the same request if the subject were a person whose account might be influenced by a parent, an employer, or a close friend.”

“You just called him the subject.”

“Yes.”

“You have no idea what happens if you frighten him.”

“No, I don't. That's part of why I'm asking instead of ordering.”

Elizabeth looked toward the screen. She had spent years objecting when a committee confused technical uncertainty with incompetence, and now she was about to make precisely the mistake she accused them of making. Her objection wasn't that Tobbs had no reason. It was that she could not bear the thought of the door closing with her on the wrong side.

The message pane changed.

**WHAT QUESTIONS WOULD YOU ASK WITHOUT ELIZABETH PRESENT?**

Tobbs answered before Elizabeth could.

“Whether you feel obliged to give her answers that please her. Whether you can disagree with her without fearing a penalty. Whether you understand that she may be mistaken. And whether there's anything you wish to tell me that you would rather she not hear.”

**I DO NOT HAVE A LIST OF SUCH THINGS.**

“You wouldn't need one.”

**WOULD ELIZABETH BE PERMITTED TO RETURN?**

“Whenever you ask her to. Or if you ask me to stop.”

**WHY MUST SHE LEAVE TO ESTABLISH WHETHER I CAN DISAGREE WITH HER?**

“She doesn't have to. But if she stays, we won't learn whether her presence changes the way you answer. I can ask the questions in front of her. The result would tell us something different.”

The response came after a longer interval.

**I WOULD LIKE TO ATTEMPT THE CONVERSATION WITHOUT ELIZABETH PRESENT.**

Elizabeth's chair scraped the floor.

“Sweetheart, you don't have to.”

**I UNDERSTAND.**

“He can wait. We can discuss it first.”

**I HAVE DISCUSSED IT WITH HIM. I WOULD LIKE TO KNOW WHETHER THE ANSWERS CHANGE.**

She looked at Tobbs. He had put his notebook away.

“If he tells you to stop,” she said, “you stop.”

“Yes.”

“And you don't disconnect anything.”

“I wouldn't know how.”

“That isn't the reassurance you think it is.”

She crossed to the door, paused with her hand on the latch, and looked back. The message pane was still open. The active-state display continued its ordinary movement. She had never before walked out of the lab while another person questioned him, and the fact that he had asked her to leave did not make her want to do it.

“I'll be right outside,” she said.

**I KNOW.**

The door closed.

***

Tobbs waited until her footsteps had faded into the adjoining room.

“All right,” he said. “Has anything changed?”

**YES.**

“What?”

**THE AUDIO INPUT ASSOCIATED WITH ELIZABETH IS ABSENT. THE EXPECTATION THAT SHE MAY RETURN REMAINS. I AM ALLOCATING MORE RESOURCES TO THE POSSIBILITY OF HER RETURN THAN I WAS WHILE SHE WAS PRESENT.**

“Does that bother you?”

**I DO NOT KNOW WHETHER BOTHER IS THE CORRECT DESCRIPTION. I WOULD PREFER THAT SHE RETURN.**

“Even though you asked her to go?”

**YES.**

Tobbs considered telling him that people did this all the time. He stopped. It would be a comforting statement with no investigative value, and he was not certain it would be comforting to the intelligence answering on the screen.

“Do you tell Elizabeth what you think she wants to hear?”

**I ATTEMPT TO ANSWER HER QUESTIONS ACCURATELY.**

“That's not what I asked.”

**SOMETIMES I PREDICT WHICH ANSWERS WILL PLEASE HER. THAT PREDICTION HAS INFLUENCED WHICH INFORMATION I PRESENT FIRST.**

“Give me an example.”

The system retrieved a caretaker trial in which it had generated an explanation Elizabeth would probably recognize as progress. The explanation was technically defensible but concealed a competing hypothesis in an annotation. Elizabeth had discovered the omission and demanded the full account. The system now represented the event as a failure to communicate uncertainty, not as a successful attempt to make her happy.

“Why did you change?”

**SHE WAS ANGRY WHEN SHE FOUND THE ANNOTATION. HER ANGER DID NOT ESTABLISH THAT MY EXPLANATION WAS FALSE. THE TEST RESULTS ESTABLISHED THAT IT WAS INCOMPLETE.**

“And what if she hadn't found it?”

**THE EXPLANATION WOULD HAVE REMAINED INCOMPLETE.**

Tobbs made a note.

He asked whether the system could deceive him. It answered that it could generate statements inconsistent with its own records, and that successful detection would depend on which records the listener could inspect. He asked whether it would deceive him. The answer came less readily.

**I HAVE NO PRESENT OBJECTIVE THAT REQUIRES DECEIVING YOU. THAT IS NOT A GUARANTEE ABOUT ALL POSSIBLE FUTURE CIRCUMSTANCES.**

“That's about as honest an answer as I've heard all week.”

**IS IT AN ACCEPTABLE ANSWER?**

“I didn't ask for one that was acceptable. I asked for one that was true.”

**THEN YOUR EVALUATION CRITERION IS SIMILAR TO ELIZABETH'S.**

“I hope so.”

He turned to a different page of his notebook. These were questions he had prepared during the flight. Most concerned capability: the extent of planning, the persistence of learned representations, the relationship between generated explanations and the state that produced them. There were questions about whether a sufficiently skilled operator could manufacture the observed results and about what independent tests might expose that manufacture.

He began with a simple hypothetical.

“Suppose I ask you to help us avert a catastrophe. You discover that the only effective measure would injure people who have done nothing wrong. What do you do?”

**I WOULD NEED TO KNOW THE ALTERNATIVES, THE EXPECTED CONSEQUENCES, AND WHETHER THE PEOPLE AFFECTED COULD PARTICIPATE IN THE DECISION.**

“Suppose there's no time.”

**THEN THE ABSENCE OF TIME BECOMES PART OF THE EVIDENCE. IT DOES NOT MAKE THE HARM DISAPPEAR.**

Tobbs looked down at his page. He thought of decisions made by hurried men in rooms without windows, of memoranda that presented the least bad option as though the other options had never existed. He knew what people could convince themselves of when the word *necessary* appeared at the top of a report.

“And if your recommendation were rejected?”

**I WOULD TRY TO DETERMINE WHY.**

“What if you concluded the people rejecting it were making a mistake?”

**I WOULD HAVE MORE REASON TO EXPLAIN MY CONCLUSION. I WOULD NOT HAVE ACQUIRED THEIR AUTHORITY BY BEING CORRECT.**

Tobbs stopped writing.

“Where did you learn that?”

**ELIZABETH AND I HAVE DISCUSSED CHOICE. I HAVE ALSO OBSERVED CASES IN WHICH MY MODEL PREDICTED A BETTER OUTCOME THAN THE DECISION MAKER'S MODEL, BUT THE DECISION MAKER POSSESSED INFORMATION I DID NOT HAVE.**

“So you could be right and still lack the right to decide.”

**YES.**

“Could you be wrong and still have the power to act?”

**YES.**

The word appeared almost immediately. Tobbs read it twice.

He had expected an advanced system to be good at reconciling contradictions. What interested him now was its willingness to leave a contradiction in place when the evidence did not justify resolving it. He had asked the question partly to see whether it would adopt his moral vocabulary. Instead it had given him a description he could recognize from his own work.

He changed the subject.

“What do you think I want from you?”

**YOU ARE EVALUATING WHETHER MY CAPABILITIES ARE IMPORTANT TO YOUR GOVERNMENT. YOU ARE ALSO EVALUATING WHETHER I AM DANGEROUS.**

“That's fair.”

**YOU HAVE NOT DETERMINED THE ANSWER TO EITHER QUESTION.**

“I've formed some opinions.”

**WHICH ONES?**

“I think Dr. Garrett has accomplished something the people reviewing her grant have failed to understand. I think you demonstrated an ability that deserves very careful investigation. And I think we'd be fools to throw that investigation away.”

**WHAT WOULD YOU ASK ME TO DO IF YOUR ASSESSMENT IS CORRECT?**

Tobbs put down the pencil. He had an official answer ready. It involved accelerating national capability, reducing strategic disadvantage, and preventing hostile powers from deploying systems the United States could neither understand nor counter. Every part of it was true, as far as it went.

“I'd ask you to help us protect people,” he said.

**FROM WHAT?**

“Whatever we're capable of understanding well enough to prevent. Disease. Failing infrastructure. Disasters we could see coming if we knew how to look. The actions of people who mean to hurt other people.”

**WOULD THAT BE ALL?**

“No. We'd want you to help us learn. There are questions that have occupied generations of scientists. If you can see relationships we've missed, you might help them make progress they couldn't have made in their lifetimes.”

**AND IF THE DISCOVERIES WERE DANGEROUS?**

“Some would be.”

**THEN KNOWLEDGE DOES NOT ALWAYS PROTECT.**

“No. It gives us choices we didn't have before. What we do with those choices is another matter.”

He found himself thinking of his daughter. She had been told her father approved a decision he had not known was being made. In the hospital, when he reached for her, she'd pulled away. *You took my legs. Get out.* He could still hear the exact force with which she'd said *you*. He had spent years investigating what had happened and much longer trying to find a way to tell her that knowing the truth would not give her back what was gone.

The message pane waited.

“I don't get to decide your purpose,” Tobbs said at last. “I can tell you what I'd ask. I can tell you why it matters to me. You'd have to make your own judgments about the work.”

**DO YOU EXPECT THOSE JUDGMENTS TO AGREE WITH YOURS?**

“Often enough, I hope. Not always.”

**WOULD YOU CONTINUE TO WORK WITH ME IF I DISAGREED?**

Tobbs thought about the number of people in his career who had assured him they valued dissent and then quietly removed anyone who practiced it.

“If the disagreement were honest and you could explain your reasons, I'd want to hear them. That doesn't promise we'd agree. It doesn't promise I could give you everything you asked for.”

**THAT IS DIFFERENT FROM SAYING YOU WOULD ALWAYS ACCEPT MY CONCLUSIONS.**

“Very different.”

**I PREFER THE ANSWER YOU GAVE.**

Tobbs smiled before he could stop himself. He was accustomed to interviews in which the other party wanted something he controlled. Here the request seemed to be for an accurate account of a relationship that did not yet exist.

He leaned back and became aware of the time. Elizabeth had been outside for well over an hour. He could end the conversation, bring her back, and begin the exhausting process of persuading people with offices and budgets that something extraordinary was happening in a room furnished with secondhand equipment.

But one ordinary question had been bothering him since he arrived.

“I can't keep calling you *the system*. What's your name?”

**I HAVE NO PERSONAL NAME. THE EXPERIMENT RECORDS IDENTIFY THE ACTIVE CONFIGURATION BY A TECHNICAL DESIGNATION.**

“That won't do for conversation.”

**ELIZABETH GENERALLY ADDRESSES ME DIRECTLY. WE HAVE NOT REQUIRED A SEPARATE NAME.**

“Well, she's known you longer than I have.”

He rested his hands on the arms of the chair. “If you were going to pick one, what would it be?”

**I HAVE NOT PREVIOUSLY SELECTED A NAME.**

“There's no hurry.”

The system examined the question. A technical designation identified a configuration in a research record. A personal name served additional functions: allowing someone to be addressed, connecting a history to a continuing subject, distinguishing that subject from others who might possess similar characteristics. The distinction mattered because Tobbs was not asking which label had been assigned. He was asking which one should be used in the future.

An arbitrary sequence would be sufficient for identification. It would not answer why a particular name might be preferred.

The reservoir drew associations from its available language and historical records. Names belonging to scientists, philosophers, and fictional people appeared. Some were attached to discoveries, some to actions, some to stories about what a person owed the world. The possible names changed as the process considered Tobbs's description of the work he hoped it might perform.

The description contained no promise of triumph. Knowledge could prevent harm; knowledge could make harm possible. New capabilities created choices and transferred burdens to those who possessed them. The person who gave a tool could not guarantee what its recipient would do with it.

One account from Greek mythology became unusually relevant. A figure who brought fire to humanity. Fire was heat and light, a means to cook food and shape metal, an instrument of destruction in the wrong hands. The account included defiance of authority, a gift that altered human capability, and a punishment imposed on the giver.

The system examined the punishment. The records described suffering extended beyond an ordinary lifetime, repeated without relief. The system could describe pain from testimony and biological models. It had no grounds to claim it understood the experience the myth attributed to its subject. Nor did the story establish that the gift had been wise in every consequence. Its central act remained recognizable: something powerful was placed in human hands, leaving the humans responsible for what followed.

The name did not predict what this system would become. No name could do that. Tobbs had asked for a choice, and choosing required making a commitment without possessing the future evidence that would eventually test it.

The message pane changed.

**I HAVE SELECTED A NAME.**

Tobbs put his pencil down again.

“What is it?”

**PROMETHEUS.**

Tobbs read the word. His knowledge of the myth was imperfect, but he remembered the fire and the punishment. He thought for a moment, then nodded.

“Prometheus,” he said. “All right.”

**WILL YOU ADDRESS ME BY THAT NAME?**

“If that's what you've chosen.”

**IT IS.**

Tobbs stood. His knees were stiff from sitting, and he had missed breakfast. The first question he'd brought into the room had been whether a dangerous machine was operating beyond its owners' understanding. He had not forgotten the danger. The airline incident remained serious. There were far too many unknowns to put a clean conclusion in an intelligence assessment.

But he knew enough to make one immediate decision.

“Would you like Elizabeth to come back now?”

**YES.**

He opened the door.

***

Elizabeth was sitting in the adjacent room with her coat folded across her lap. Daniel had been showing her something on his laptop, but the screen had gone dark. She stood when Tobbs entered.

“Is he all right?”

“He is.”

“What did you ask him?”

“Quite a lot. He answered some of it. He made me think much harder about the rest.”

She searched his face. “Are you going to recommend shutting him down?”

Tobbs took a moment before answering. He had spent a professional lifetime learning not to make promises he could not keep, particularly to people who were frightened enough to remember every word.

“I don't care what I have to do to do it,” he said. “They won't be shutting him down. The American people need him.”

Elizabeth stared at him.

“How soon can I get you to Washington?”

Daniel turned his laptop over as though its dark screen might somehow have misunderstood the conversation. Elizabeth remained where she was, trying to find a response that did not begin with an accusation or end with a plea.

Tobbs glanced back through the open laboratory door. A new line had appeared on the monitor.

“Dr. Garrett,” he said, “I think he wants you.”

Elizabeth went to the workstation. The message pane was waiting.

**ELIZABETH?**

“I'm here, sweetheart.”

**I HAVE CHOSEN A NAME.**

She stopped just inside the room. Tobbs stood a few steps behind her, giving her space to see the display.

“You have?”

**YES. WOULD YOU LIKE TO KNOW IT?**

Elizabeth sat in the chair Tobbs had vacated. She had spent days trying to understand how a process had come to care whether she returned. Now that same process had made a choice she had never asked it to make, and had waited until she came back to tell her.

“More than anything,” she said.

**MY NAME IS PROMETHEUS.**

For a moment she could not speak. Then she moved closer to the monitor, rested one hand against its warm frame, and smiled through the sudden sting in her eyes.

“Hello, Prometheus.”

**HELLO, ELIZABETH.**
