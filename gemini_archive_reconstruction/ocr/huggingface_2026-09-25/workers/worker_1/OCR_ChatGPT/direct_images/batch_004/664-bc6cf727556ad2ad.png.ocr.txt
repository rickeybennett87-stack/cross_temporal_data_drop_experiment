Abstract

Precipitation is characterized by substantial natural
variability, including on regional and decadal scales. This
relatively large variability poses a grand challenge in
assessing the significance of anthropogenically forced
precipitation changes. Here we use multiple large ensembles
of climate change experiments to evaluate whether, on
regional scales, anthropogenic changes in decadal
precipitation mean state are distinguishable. Here,
distinguishable means the anthropogenic change is outside
the range expected from natural variability. Relative to the
