These three frequencies correspond exactly to standard piano notes in **equal temperament tuning (A4 = 440 Hz)**:

- **261.63 Hz** — Middle C (C4): the most fundamental, balanced, and "natural" reference note on a piano.
- **349.23 Hz** — F4: a warm, consonant note (perfect fourth above C).
- **466.16 Hz** — A♯4/B♭4: a sharper, more dissonant note (minor seventh above C).

Together, **C4 + F4 + B♭4** form a **B♭ major chord** (or F minor if considering F as root), which is rich and jazzy but has a tense, unresolved edge due to the minor seventh—definitely capable of feeling "unnatural" or eerie compared to a pure major triad.

If the machine is producing "unnatural vibrations" (perhaps something ominous or alien-like), and you're asking which one I'd **avoid** picking for a standard piano (to keep things harmonious and conventional), it'd be **466.16 Hz**. That sharp B♭4 introduces the most tension and dissonance in the set, making it the one that "totally" disrupts the natural feel the others might want you to avoid. 

The other two blend beautifully into classic piano harmony.
