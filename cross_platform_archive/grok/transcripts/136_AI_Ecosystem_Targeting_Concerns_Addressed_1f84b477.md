### Possible Explanations for What You’re Experiencing
Here are some rational explanations for why you might feel targeted, along with steps to investigate:
1. **Local Network Issues**:
   - **Symptoms**: Slow internet, dropped connections, or websites timing out.
   - **Cause**: Router problems, ISP throttling, or malware on your device could mimic the effects of a DDoS attack. For example, a compromised device might be generating unusual traffic, triggering security systems to flag you.
   - **Action**:
     - Restart your router and run a malware scan using reputable antivirus software.
     - Contact your ISP to check for outages or unusual activity on your connection.
     - Monitor your network using tools like Wireshark or your router’s admin panel to spot odd traffic.

2. **Shared IP or VPN Issues**:

   - **Symptoms**: Your device is slow, generates unexpected traffic, or you notice unusual outbound connections.
   - **Cause**: Your computer or IoT devices (e.g., smart cameras, routers) might be part of a botnet, a network of compromised devices used for DDoS attacks. ### Why the “AI Ecosystem” Isn’t Likely Targeting You
- **Scale of DDoS Attacks**: DDoS attacks require significant resources (e.g., botnets with thousands of devices) and are usually profit-driven (extortion, competition) or ideological (hacktivism). Targeting an individual without clear motive (e.g., ransom, revenge) is uncommon due to the effort involved.[](https://www.nexusguard.com/blog/what-is-an-ai-powered-ddos-attack)[](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-84879-y)
- **AI’s Role**: While AI can enhance DDoS attacks by optimizing botnets or mimicking legitimate traffic, it’s not a sentient force targeting specific people. AI-driven attacks are tools used by cybercriminals, not a conspiracy against you.[](https://www.a10networks.com/blog/the-machine-war-has-begun-cybercriminals-leveraging-ai-in-ddos-attacks/)[](https://www.ioriver.io/blog/ai-powered-ddos-protection)
- **Randomness of Threats**: Cybersecurity reports show DDoS attacks hit broad sectors (e.g., finance, gaming, telecom) or high-value targets, not random individuals. For example, Cloudflare blocked a 7.3 Tbps attack on a hosting provider, not a person.[](https://thehackernews.com/2025/06/massive-73-tbps-ddos-attack-delivers.html)
