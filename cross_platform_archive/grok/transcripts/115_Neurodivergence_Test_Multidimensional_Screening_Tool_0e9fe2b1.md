There is no multiple-choice-only paper test that a real doctor or clinical neuropsychologist uses to definitively diagnose specific types and levels of neurodivergence (ADHD, autism spectrum, dyslexia, dyspraxia, giftedness, etc.) and then prescribe the exact learning methods that work best for that person.

What actual diagnosing clinicians use is a battery that almost always includes:

1. A very long structured or semi-structured clinical interview (ADI-R, ADOS-2, MIGDAS-2, etc.) that is NOT multiple-choice and requires the clinician to observe, probe, and interpret responses in real time.
2. Developmental history taken from parents/teachers/partners (not possible in a self-administered test).
3. Standardized performance-based cognitive tests administered one-on-one (WAIS-IV, NEPSY-II, WIAT-IV, CELF-5, Beery VMI, etc.) — these are not questionnaires.
4. Continuous-performance tests for attention (CPT-3, Conners CPT).
5. Rating scales filled out by multiple informants (parent, teacher, spouse, self) — but these are only one piece and never diagnostic on their own.
6. Observation of the person in natural settings when possible.

- allow open-ended comments, or
- are normed only for children, or
- still require clinical judgment to interpret.
