## 🧠 How They Work (vs. Rotary)
- **Vibrating/Impact Tools**: Use a rapid back-and-forth or up-and-down motion to *strike* the surface with a carbide-tipped stylus. This leaves a mark by impacting the metal, similar to a tiny chisel controlled by a motor. citeturn0search1  
- **Rotary Tools**: Spin cutting bits at high speed to *remove material* — great for deep cuts but less ideal for fine surface lettering without chatter. citeturn0search13

---

## 🪶 Tips for Engraving Metal with a Vibrating Tool
- Wear eye protection and a dust mask — the tip impact can throw tiny metal particles.  
- Clamp your work solidly so it doesn’t move under the impacts.  
- Use appropriate carbide or diamond tips for harder metals like stainless steel.  
- For more precise, machine-like engravings (text/logos), consider *diamond drag/drag-stylus* methods used in CNC machines (non-spinning but more controlled). citeturn0search12

## Why these work better than spinning tools
A rotary or burr:
- *grabs the metal*
- wants to follow the grain or slip
- can jump around if your hand isn’t *perfectly still*

A vibrating pen:
- taps straight up-and-down
- doesn’t pull or tug sideways
- lets you control the groove like a chisel

Think of the difference like:
- spinning bit = *electric knife*
- vibrating tip = *tiny powered chisel*

For hand lettering, the chisel-style feedback is way easier to manage.
