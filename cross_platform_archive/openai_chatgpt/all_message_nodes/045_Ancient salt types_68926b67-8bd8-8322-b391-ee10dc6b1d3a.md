In ancient times, the phrase **“to salt the earth”** was often more symbolic than agricultural, but when salt was actually used, it was usually **common rock salt or sea salt** — **sodium chloride** — in its natural, unrefined form.

### Types of Salt Likely Used:
1. **Rock Salt (Halite)**  
   - Mined or gathered from dry lakebeds or salt deposits.
   - Common in arid regions like the Middle East (e.g., the Dead Sea region).
   - Crude and mixed with other minerals.

2. **Sea Salt**  
   - Harvested from evaporated seawater.
   - Used in coastal areas like Phoenicia, Greece, and Rome.
   - Less refined than modern table salt, but still sodium chloride-rich.

3. **Salt from Salt Flats or Brine Pools**  
   - Areas like **Lake Asal** in Djibouti or **Lake Urmia** in Persia would have provided briny salt crusts.
   - These were scraped and used as crude salt.

### Historical Examples of “Salting the Earth”:
- **Carthage (146 BCE)**: After Rome destroyed Carthage, legend says they salted the ground so nothing would grow — but this is **likely symbolic**. No solid evidence suggests they actually used tons of salt; it was probably a metaphor for total destruction.
  
- **Shechem and Abimelech (Judges 9:45)**: The Bible mentions Abimelech salting Shechem — possibly literally, but again likely as a **ritual curse** on the land.

### Why Sodium Chloride?
Because:
- It was widely available.
- It was known to be destructive in excess (too much salt kills plant life and soil microbes).
- It had **ritual, economic, and symbolic** power — salt was sacred, valuable, and tied to purification and preservation.

> 📌 **Important**: They did **not** use specialty salts like potassium nitrate ("saltpeter"), Epsom salt, or modern chemical salts — those are later discoveries or refinements.
