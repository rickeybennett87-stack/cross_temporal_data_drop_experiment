Wikipedia strongly discourages writing your own biography because that creates a conflict of interest. A biography also needs to satisfy Wikipedia’s **notability** standard, which generally requires substantial coverage of you in reliable, independent secondary sources. Your own website, social-media pages, material you wrote, and your organization’s website generally don’t establish that by themselves. citeturn0search0turn0search1

The safest route is:

1. Log into Wikipedia and create a **Draft**, rather than publishing directly into the encyclopedia.
2. Build the article almost entirely from independent published sources. Every significant factual claim, especially biographical ones, should be traceable to a source.
3. Write in third-person encyclopedia language, not résumé/website language. So instead of “Bennett developed a groundbreaking new theory...,” you'd need something like “In 2026, *Publication X* reported that Bennett proposed...” followed by the citation.
4. Disclose on the draft’s Talk page that **you are the subject of the proposed article**. That makes the conflict transparent rather than trying to hide it.
5. Submit the draft through Wikipedia’s **Articles for Creation** process so an uninvolved editor can review it.

The biggest question actually comes *before* writing: **Do we have enough independent sources about you to survive an Articles for Deletion discussion?** Wikipedia specifically distinguishes between information that merely verifies facts and independent coverage that demonstrates notability. citeturn0search0turn0search2

Given that you're talking about your own page, I would **not start by writing the biography**. Start by assembling the evidence.
