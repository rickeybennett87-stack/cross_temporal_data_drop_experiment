Domino, whose real name is **Neena Thurman**, is a fascinating character from Marvel Comics who first appeared in the early 1990s—not quite the "early era" of comics like the Golden or Silver Age, but still a pivotal time in expanding representation and complexity in superhero narratives.

### 🃏 Who Is Domino?
- **First Appearance**: *The New Mutants #98* (1991), though this was actually an imposter named Copycat. The real Domino debuted in *X-Force #8* (1992).
- **Creators**: Fabian Nicieza and Rob Liefeld
- **Affiliations**: X-Force, X-Men, Six Pack, Weapon X, and more
- **Powers**: Subconscious telekinetic manipulation of probability—essentially, she makes her own luck. She’s also a master marksman and martial artist.

### 🎨 Visual Identity
Rob Liefeld designed her with a striking black-and-white motif, inspired by the game dominoes. Her pale skin and black eye mark became iconic, symbolizing her chaotic yet calculated nature.

### 🧬 Origin Story
Domino was the product of **Project: Armageddon**, a government experiment aiming to recreate the Super Soldier serum. Instead of brute strength, she gained probability-altering powers. Raised by a priest after escaping the project, she later became a mercenary and operative for various agencies.

### 🖤 Representation and Race
Domino is often mistaken as a "token Black character" due to her portrayal by Zazie Beetz in *Deadpool 2* (2018), where the character was reimagined as a Black woman. However, in the comics, Domino is depicted with chalk-white skin and is not canonically Black. The film adaptation made a bold and refreshing choice, but it diverges from her original comic portrayal.

If you're exploring early Black superheroes in comics, characters like **Black Panther (1966)**, **Luke Cage (1972)**, and **Storm (1975)** were more direct representations of Black identity in the early eras. Domino’s cinematic portrayal adds to the evolving conversation about race and representation in superhero media.
