## What Zenodo is  
Zenodo is an **open science repository** created by **CERN** and funded through the **European Commission’s OpenAIRE project**. Its purpose is to provide a **catch‑all archive** for research outputs across all disciplines, formats, and stages of the research lifecycle. 

Key characteristics:

- **Accepts any research artifact** — data, software, papers, presentations, posters, workflows, and more.  
- **Assigns a DOI** to every upload, making it citable and persistent.  
- **Supports versioning**, allowing you to publish updated versions under the same DOI family.  
- **Allows up to 50 GB per upload** with no restrictions on file type or format.  
- **Supports restricted, embargoed, and closed content** for pre‑publication workflows. 

---

## Why Zenodo matters  
Zenodo is designed to ensure **no researcher is excluded** from open data practices due to institutional or national limitations. It provides:

- **Long‑term preservation** using CERN’s digital library infrastructure.  
- **Open access compliance** for EU‑funded research.  
- **GitHub integration** for automatic archiving of software releases.  
- **Global reach**, accepting research from any discipline and any country. 

---

## What you can upload  
Zenodo welcomes **all digital artifacts**, including:

- Datasets (e.g., linguistic datasets, biological datasets)  
- Software packages (e.g., ESPResSo simulation software)  
- Presentations and training materials  
- Tables and supplementary materials from published papers  
- Conference materials and institutional outputs  

Examples from recent uploads include **molecular dynamics software**, **omics experimental design presentations**, and **biodiversity datasets**. 

---

## How Zenodo supports research workflows  
Zenodo is built to support the **entire research lifecycle**, not just final publications:

- Upload early and keep items **restricted** until publication.  
- Share **protected links** with reviewers.  
- Automatically **open embargoed content** when the associated paper is published.  
- Maintain **complete reproducibility** by storing *all* digital artifacts, not just the final paper.
