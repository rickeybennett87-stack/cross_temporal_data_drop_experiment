**Quick Answer:**  
A safe rule of thumb for a 1099 contractor in California is to set aside **30–35% of gross income** for taxes. This covers **federal income tax, California state income tax, and self-employment tax (Social Security + Medicare)**.  

---

### 🔎 Breakdown of Tax Obligations for 1099 Contractors in California

#### 1. **Federal Self-Employment Tax (Social Security + Medicare)**
- **Rate:** 15.3% of net earnings (after business expenses)  
  - 12.4% Social Security (up to $168,600 in 2025)  
  - 2.9% Medicare (no cap)  
  - Additional 0.9% Medicare tax if income > $200,000 (single filer)  

#### 2. **Federal Income Tax**
- Progressive brackets ranging from **10% to 37%** depending on taxable income.  
- Most self-employed individuals fall in the **12–24% range**.  
- Deductible business expenses reduce taxable income.  

#### 3. **California State Income Tax**
- California has **progressive rates from 1% to 12.3%**.  
- For most contractors, expect **6–9%** effective state tax.  
- Additional 1% “mental health services tax” applies if income exceeds $1 million.  

---

### 📊 Practical Deduction Estimate
- **Self-Employment Tax:** ~15%  
- **Federal Income Tax:** ~12–24% (average ~15%)  
- **California State Tax:** ~6–9%  

➡️ **Total Effective Range:** ~30–35% of gross income (after expenses).  

---

### ✅ Actionable Recommendation
- **Set aside 30–35% of gross 1099 income** each month.  
- Pay **quarterly estimated taxes** to IRS and California FTB to avoid penalties.  
- Use tools like the [IRS Tax Withholding Estimator](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/tax-withholding-estimator) or California’s [FTB guidance](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/personal/filing-situations/employee-or-independent-contractor.html) for more precise calculations.  
- Track deductible expenses carefully (home office, mileage, supplies) to reduce taxable income.
